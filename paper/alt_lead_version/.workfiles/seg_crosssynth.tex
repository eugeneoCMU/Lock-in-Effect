\subsection{Cross-Design Synthesis: The Symmetric Companion Test}\label{sec:robustness-synthesis}

The symmetric companion test estimates the hazard framework's survival structure on a synthetic population. It materially updates the cross-design picture. Path B recovers 106.0\% of the benchmark with no real Freddie Mac data at all (96.9\% on the shared accounting basis, whose common netting applies equally here), close to its 107.0\% real-data recovery at the same in-sample calibration point. The raw levels correlation, however, drops from $+0.190$ to $-0.06$ at lag 0 (a levels comparison; neither value survives detrending, Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). That 106.0\% is a recovery level, and this paper demotes recovery levels everywhere else for a reason. The mechanism-removed leg on the same synthetic population ($\beta_1 = 0$, nothing else changed) recovers 95.6\%, so the test identifies a differential of 10.4 points, or \$79.3 billion, not the 106.0\% level (\texttt{synthetic\_companion\_null}). That differential is of the same order as the 9.2-point real-book gap between the central leg's 107.0\% and the $\beta_1 = 0$ null's 97.8\%. Section~\ref{sec:identification} treats that gap as too small to license a level-based reading. A superseded cross-basis figure put that gap at 18.3 points, which was an error of basis rather than of arithmetic. It differenced a standalone-scorer central leg against a shared-basis null. On any single basis the real-book gap is 9.2 points at the in-sample calibration point and 5.6 points at the off-window floor, so the synthetic differential slightly exceeds its real-book counterpart. The synthetic recovery is floor-dominated like the real one. This decomposes the tension. The household-choice paradigm needed real covariates to reach 59.3\%, while the loan-level paradigm reaches comparable aggregate recovery with none. At the path level both paradigms still depend on real structure for correct timing. I treat the aggregate-level contrast of Sections~\ref{sec:hazard} and~\ref{sec:conclusion} as corroborated by this result on its differential rather than substantially strengthened by its level, and the path-level claim as still open. The paradigm reading itself is withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign}); nothing here is offered in support of it.
