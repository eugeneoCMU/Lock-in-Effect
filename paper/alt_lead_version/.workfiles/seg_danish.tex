\subsection{The Danish Market-Value-Repurchase Alternative}\label{sec:abm-danish}

\subsubsection{The transplant}

A third alternative acts on the payoff rule itself, not on portability or the redemption cap. Section~\ref{sec:abm-results} shows that no synthetic-population household-choice specification tested here explains the majority of the cap shortfall. Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign} confines that finding to the synthetic population: its recalibrated real-covariate variant crossed the pre-committed threshold past which the paradigm reading cannot be cleanly maintained. The question is therefore how much of that trap the par-payoff rule itself causes, rather than household behavior under any institutional regime. The Danish market-value repurchase system severs the link between market rates and payoff cost, which lets me ask that quantitatively. The operative specification calibrates each framework's Danish response against \citet{berger2026}'s separately estimated moving and refinance-in-place channels. It then transplants them onto the U.S.\ loan population, so that only the payoff rule changes. \citet{berger2026} is a July 2026 SSRN working draft, and the single unrefereed source this channel rests on. The paper's finding on this question is an institutional gap of $+\$61.2$ billion (Table~\ref{tab:danish}).\footnote{\label{fn:manifest}CPR means below aggregate the 42 active QT months (June 2022--November 2025), and the institutional wedge is those months' mean cohort-weighted Danish-minus-U.S.\ simulated CPR difference. This operative specification supersedes an initial mechanism-substitution that had implied a much larger gap. That superseded figure, and the frozen run manifests underlying every ABM headline, are catalogued in the replication package's ledger.}

\subsubsection{The two estimated channels}

The recalibration rests on two behaviorally distinct channels. \citet{berger2026}---my source for the Danish institutional mechanism itself---estimate them separately, and a single extrapolated mechanism would conflate them. The moving channel is whether removing the par-payoff penalty changes how often households relocate. It is well-identified and close to flat in the coupon gap: Danish households move at an unconditional 3.2\% per year regardless of how far their coupon sits below market \citep[\S4.6, structural parameters in tab.~3]{berger2026}. Their Danish administrative microdata put that slope near zero and not consistently signed. A 100 bp \emph{decrease} in the yield gap moves the annual moving rate by about $+14$ basis points under their specification, and about $-19$ under \citeauthor{fonseca2024}'s control set \citep[\S3.3.2]{berger2026}. The U.S. moving-hazard slope, by contrast, is 0.57--1.20 percentage points per year per 100 bp of coupon gap \citep{fonseca2024}. The refinance-in-place channel is whether a market-discount buyback induces discount-buying independent of moving. It is substantially a Danish tax artifact: buybacks are attractive there because mortgage interest is deductible at a flat 33\% rate and realized capital gains on the buyback are tax-exempt. Under U.S. tax treatment---a 22\% mortgage-interest deduction and a 15\% capital-gains tax on the discount, per Berger et al.'s own counterfactual---the channel loses that shield. Their general-equilibrium counterfactual \citep[\S4.10.2]{berger2026} finds that introducing the option raises the equilibrium mortgage rate by about 20 basis points on average, 16--23 across their robustness variants. They read that as leaving equilibrium rates virtually unchanged. That estimate is itself draft-sensitive: the January 2026 version of the same paper reported about one basis point. That is one more reason this channel is carried as an unrefereed import rather than a settled magnitude. I read that characterization differently, though I do not adjudicate it. On the ordinary reading a discount extinguishment is cancellation-of-debt income at ordinary rates, IRC \S 61(a)(11) and Treas.\ Reg.\ \S 1.61-12(c). But \S 108(a)(1)(B) and \S 108(a)(1)(E) exclude it for insolvent borrowers and for qualified principal-residence indebtedness respectively, so the treatment is borrower-state-dependent, not uniform in either direction. Post-TCJA, the assumed interest deduction also overstates most households' marginal benefit. Read as a directional bracket, both considerations push the U.S.-transplant channel \emph{below} Berger et al.'s already-small estimate. Neither touches the realized Danish evidence, which is measured under Danish tax.

Recalibrating both frameworks against these two estimated channels first requires a choice about the moving channel's \emph{level}. The choice arises because Berger et al.'s identified fact about the moving margin is one of slope: Danish moving is flat in the coupon gap under market-value payoff. The 3.2\% per year, by contrast, is Denmark's descriptive unconditional moving rate, not an estimate of how often U.S. households would move under a Danish payoff rule. Importing that level onto the U.S. loan population---the \emph{Danish-level} anchor---therefore bundles two changes, the payoff rule and Danish baseline mobility itself. It produces Danish legs whose mean CPR (3.36--3.39\%, Table~\ref{tab:danish}) sits \emph{below} the 4\% turnover floor both frameworks impose on the U.S. side (Appendix~\ref{sec:method-frictions} and Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). Death, divorce, and forced relocation do not become rarer because the payoff rule changed. A rule-only counterfactual on the same U.S. households therefore cannot prepay below that floor. The construction that isolates the rule is the \emph{U.S.-intercept} anchor. It keeps the identified flatness but anchors the level at the production U.S. hazard evaluated at zero rate gap---turnover floor, seasoning baseline, burnout, and covariate terms all retained, only the rate-gap suppression switched off---and it carries the same U.S.-transplant refinance-in-place channel. I adopt it as the production reading, the only one consistent with the floor's stated semantics. I retain the Danish-level anchor as a labeled bracketing case in which U.S. households are assumed to adopt Danish baseline mobility along with the Danish rule. Holding the floor fixed while importing only the Danish moving slope treats baseline turnover and rate-elastic moving as separable channels. That separability is motivated by the floor's semantics. It is not identified in the data available here.

\subsubsection{The anchor choice and the sign of the gap}

The anchor choice determines the sign of the institutional gap. Under the shared-accounting hybrid pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-hybrid}), the U.S.-intercept anchor yields a Danish mean CPR of 5.61\% against the locked-in U.S. leg's 4.76\% and a gap of $+\$61.2$ billion (8.0\% of the benchmark) at Berger et al.'s general-equilibrium best estimate of negligible refinance-in-place; the U.S. leg reproduces the committed production artifacts exactly. That estimate is a price statement (an equilibrium-rate effect of about 20 basis points), not a near-zero buyback \emph{volume}. Realized Danish data contradict the near-zero volume: over 2022M07--2023M12 deep-discount ($\leq 2\%$ coupon) Danish callable bonds redeemed at 26.4\% per year against a 4--5\% baseline. The extraordinary component is at least 22\% per year after a generous scheduled-amortization allowance (run \texttt{danish\_redemption\_validation}; Danmarks Nationalbank securities statistics, exact stock--transaction closure on the coupon-blocked universe). The transplant imports the payoff rule alone: not the Balance Principle's series-level match funding, the large tap-issued series carrying Denmark's liquidity, the advisory distribution prompting conversions, or the Danish product mix --- each a held-fixed feature of the U.S. leg, not a measured invariance, and one, the large interest-only share, now carrying a magnitude. I therefore report the rule-only gap as a band over the refinance-in-place contribution, not a point at zero: swept in 0.25\%-CPR steps under the production anchor, the gap runs from $+\$61.2$ billion at 0\% to $+\$256.8$ billion at 3\% (run \texttt{danish\_refi\_finegrid}; slope $\approx \$65$ billion per CPR point). The large interest-only share cuts scheduled amortization, the null's largest component, and so moves the shortfall against the payoff rule; the direction was already conceded here, so what run \texttt{danish\_interest\_only\_share} adds is the crossing. Zeroing scheduled amortization on a share $\chi$ of the Danish leg alone cuts the face gap by $\chi$ times \$198.67 billion, the U.S. leg's scheduled path over the window and this paper's own proxy for the scheduled component. I zero it on the Danish leg alone because scoring it in both legs would neutralize it exactly, and the U.S. leg's effective zero stays held fixed. The gap crosses zero at an interest-only share of 30.8\%. Deferred principal survives to prepay: carrying it forward at the Danish leg's own 5.61\% speed returns \$9.1 billion of extra early roll-off at a 45\% share and lifts the crossing to 34.3\%; the offset is reported beside the first-order effect rather than folded into it, and it was the one limb I could not sign in advance. Denmark's share sits above both crossings: Danmarks Nationalbank puts interest-only loans at 45\% of outstanding mortgage volumes \citep{nationalbanken2020}, at which the face gap is $-\$19.2$ billion after that feedback. Three limits travel with the 45\% wherever it is read. It is pre-window (February 2020 against a June 2022--November 2025 window). Danish ``interest-only'' is deferred amortisation (\emph{afdragsfrihed}), conventionally capped at ten years, so it is the share of volume currently \emph{in} an interest-only period and not a permanent product feature. Its population is whole-market outstanding volumes rather than the owner-occupied thirty-year callable segment this transplant models. The crossing carries none of them: it is arithmetic on the committed chain and needs no Danish input, which is why I lead with it. This is an accounting-layer calculation: the hazard is untouched and only the amortization path moves, so it establishes that the rule-only gap survives the product mix only below that crossing, not what a re-simulated interest-only book would return. The same leg reads in duration units. At each leg's mean CPR through the identical calculator, the rule-only transplant shortens the book from Path B's 9.7 years to 9.1 at the window's open and from 8.7 to 8.2 at its close --- roughly 0.6 years. The Danish-level bracketing leg runs the other way, to 10.8 and 9.6 (Table~\ref{tab:wal}). Duration separates the two anchors in the same direction the dollars do, without resting on the incidence choice the dollar metric cannot settle. The realized-implied anchor prices at $+\$1{,}212$ billion, beyond the rejected partial-equilibrium ceiling. Four confounds make it an upper anchor on the transplant, not an estimate of it. The realized rate is gross, so conversions bundle in; it includes non-household collateral; it is matched to the environment, not to the book; and it is a Danish-tax realization of an option whose U.S.-transplant value the tax attenuation is meant to shrink. Both tax regimes read off this one axis, worth naming because the sweep parameterizes refinance-in-place CPR, not a tax code: a regime enters only through the refinancing it induces. The U.S.-tax regime sits at \citeauthor{berger2026}'s general-equilibrium best estimate of $\approx$0\% refinance-in-place and gives $+\$61.2$ billion; the Danish-tax realization sits at the realized-implied 22.4\% per year and gives $+\$1{,}212$ billion, above the swept range entirely. They are not equally credentialed (the second carries those four confounds), but quoting only the first would present a tax-attenuated anchor as though it were the institutional gap itself. This returns to a construction the pipeline already contained: leg for leg, the U.S.-intercept hybrid coincides numerically with the NPV-identity hybrid the replication package's technical documentation records from before the Berger recalibration, the buyback-at-market-value identity zeroing the effective rate gap --- computationally the zero-gap U.S. hazard. What it lacked, and Berger et al.'s moving-flatness estimate supplies, is empirical evidence that Danish-rule moving is flat in the gap. The sign is invariant under this anchor, and it could not have come out otherwise. The specification defines the Danish moving hazard as the production U.S. hazard at a zero rate gap. The U.S. book's gap is negative in essentially every cell over essentially the whole window (99.53\% of exposure below the window-minimum market rate, Section~\ref{sec:identification}), and the hazard is monotone in it. The zero-gap leg must therefore prepay faster, the Danish trapped balance come in lower (\$687.8 billion against \$749.0 billion), the gap be positive. Sweeping refinance-in-place from 0\% to the rejected partial-equilibrium ceiling ($\approx$18\% CPR) only adds Danish prepayment, so the sweep's sign-robustness is forced by construction too: the gap is positive at every point, rising to $+\$1{,}038.9$ billion at the ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:gapsweep}). I retain both as wiring checks and read neither as evidence. That premise is an inequality about this episode, not a property of the design: the book is closed at 2017--2021 originations and the window's market rate stood above essentially all of its coupons in essentially every month. Where a rate gap is mixed in sign (another cycle, a book open to new originations at the prevailing rate), it fails, and the sign becomes a composition question this design does not answer, the elasticity being imported, not estimated; neither this sign nor the marginal transports without re-checking it. What is not forced is the magnitude: \$61.2 billion, 8.0\% of the benchmark, is what the recalibration actually delivers, and the Danish-level anchor below shows that a different and defensible transplant choice reverses the sign outright. This positive gap is the lock-in marginal of Section~\ref{sec:hazard-interp} from the counterfactual side. The $\beta_1 = 0$ null switches off the same rate-gap suppression, so the rule-only Danish counterfactual and the paper's lock-in contribution at the in-sample calibration point are one object on two accounting legs. The legs read $+\$61.2$ billion here, where the Danish leg compounds its own counterfactual balances, and $+\$70.3$ billion as the U.S.-leg marginal. At the 0\% edge of the swept band they nearly coincide, a property of that edge and of the in-sample calibration, not of the counterfactual. On that second leg the Danish magnitude inherits every conditionality the marginal carries, the baseline seasoning convention and the floor's age transport among them, so the transplant supplies no independent information about the payoff rule: it re-denominates a quantity this paper has already conditioned, conditional in exactly the same ways. The near-equality is about the demoted in-sample point, not the paper's headline. Against the $+\$42.6$ billion off-window marginal the Danish rule-only gap is about 44\% larger, not equal, and both legs were run at the production floor. Re-run at the off-window floor itself, it is $+\$28.2$ billion, 3.7\% of the benchmark, 34\% below the off-window marginal (run \texttt{danish\_offwindow\_floor}; the U.S. leg reproduces the committed off-window central exactly). No equality survives at matched floors. Under the Danish-level anchor, by contrast, the gap is $-\$99.9$ billion (U.S. 4.76\%, Danish 3.39\% mean CPR), crossing zero at a 1.4\% CPR refinance-in-place contribution; the fragility reflects the bundled mobility-culture change. The ABM's recalibrated run under that anchor shows $-\$728.4$ billion (crossing at 12.6\%), but its U.S. leg is over-predicted against the empirical benchmark (11.76\% simulated mean CPR under the Berger run's own calibration-stage freeze versus 5.14\% empirical (ABM basis); cf.\ the production tag's 11.68\%, Section~\ref{sec:abm-results}). Under the U.S.-intercept anchor its direction follows a fortiori without a run: its lock-in penalty only ever suppresses moving, so its zero-gap intercept exceeds its locked-in U.S. leg month by month and its rule-only gap is positive by construction. Table~\ref{tab:danish} collects all four specifications.
