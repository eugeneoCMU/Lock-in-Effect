\subsection{Identification: the Marginal over the Null}\label{sec:identification}

\subsubsection{The estimand}

The design does not identify the aggregate recovery level, which is floor-dominated. The $\beta_1 = 0$ null switches the elasticity off and holds baseline turnover at its observed level. It already recovers 85.7\% of the benchmark on the shared accounting basis at the headline off-window floor, and 88.7\% at the in-sample calibration point (Section~\ref{sec:hazard-interp}; standalone figures in Table~\ref{tab:bases}). What it does identify is the \emph{marginal} of the central Path B run over that null, writing $R$ for recovered trapped liquidity,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marginal}
\text{lock-in marginal} \;=\; R_{\text{central}} - R_{\beta_1 = 0},
\end{equation}
which is basis-invariant: the shared-accounting netting is common to both legs (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-hybrid}). Its value moves with the baseline turnover floor, the design's dominant level-setter. That floor is calibrated in-window on the very cohorts the marginal decomposes. The headline therefore measures that floor off that population: $+\$42.6$ billion, $+5.6$ points of the benchmark, at the defensible off-window floor of Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}, and $+4.3$ to $+6.8$ points across its range. In window the same difference is $+\$70.3$ billion, or $+9.2$ points. The recovery \emph{level} moves with the floor too, so I quote it at both calibrations, never a level from one floor beside a marginal from another. The central leg recovers 91.3\% of the benchmark on the shared basis at the off-window floor (88.2--93.7\% across its range) against 97.9\% at the in-window calibration. The null recovers 85.7\% against 88.7\%. The concave-gap variant, the only other leg re-run off window, recovers 99.8\% central against a 94.8\% null on the standalone scorer. Every other estimator level here---Path~A, the full-book and composed estimators, the covariate-prior and elasticity bands, the Ginnie overlay, the Danish legs---was run in window only. Any level without a floor label should therefore be read at the in-window 4.0\% calibration, the configuration I call the \emph{in-sample calibration point} throughout. Differencing does not immunize the marginal against non-stationary baselines. The baseline multiplies the very rate-gap channel the null switches off, so a break in $h_0$ scales the marginal rather than cancelling from it. Under a $\pm 20\%$ multiplicative break from January 2023 onward---imposed identically on both legs, fitting nothing to the benchmark---it runs from $+5.0$ to $+12.1$ points around the committed $+9.2$ (bit-exact at the unit factor). A $\pm 20\%$ break in the turnover floor alone moves it the other way, to $+6.6$ through $+10.4$ points. Every perturbation leaves it inside the calibration box and positive: what survives a baseline break is box membership, not the point value.

Seven qualifications fix its evidential content. First, the identified content is the marginal's \emph{bounded range}, neither its point magnitude nor its sign. The box spans $+2.1$ to $+13.2$ points. Across the empirically plausible floor range the marginal spans $+5.0$ to $+12.6$ points over the full band, $+10.8$ to $+5.5$ at the central elasticity, moving to $+11.25$ under the competing-risks floor form. Censoring moves with the floor's level. At the headline 4.991\% off-window floor the central leg's hazard is pinned to the turnover floor in 68.8\% of the 1,683,124 evaluated loan-months and the $\beta_1 = 0$ leg in 35.8\%, against 36.3\% and 14.3\% at the in-sample calibration point. The central share runs 60.0\% to 77.3\% across that floor's defensible range. In most loan-months at the headline calibration, then, the elasticity no longer sets the hazard, and its contribution to the marginal is truncated at the floor. These are unweighted loan-month counts, though, not balance-weighted shares: they do not say what fraction of the dollar marginal the uncensored months carry. Wherever the null leg is pinned, switching the elasticity off cannot raise the hazard at all. The marginal is thus floor-level sensitive under the production max form. It is form-robust at roughly $+9$ to $+11$ points at the production floor (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}), but has no form-robust point at the off-window anchors, where the two forms return $+5.6$ and $+11.2$. That leaves the form-conditional range $+3.5$ to $+13.1$ points as the defensible statement. Its endpoints are those of a measured mixture curve in the floor's strictly-involuntary share, rising to $+9.7$ points by $\omega = 0.25$ and plateauing near $+11.2$. The paper's own floor semantics point at the hull's upper region, and the $\omega = 0$ headline is the curve's conservative endpoint. Its upper end comes from the additive form at the 4.695\% floor and the band's 7.7\% high edge, whose central leg recovers 61.2\% of the benchmark. Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor} states that it should not be read as an equally credentialed member of the range. The hull holds each floor read at its point estimate, so it is not the paper's widest disclosed interval. Propagating the floor reads' own cluster-bootstrap sampling error puts the max-form member at $+2.3$ to $+9.1$ points after wild-cluster correction (percentile read $+3.0$ to $+8.0$; Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}), and leaves it open below $+4.3$ on an age-standardized read (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}). The floor read and the loan/stratum resample are reported side by side, not maximized over. Convolved as independent draws the pair spans $+2.8$ to $+9.0$ points (computed on the Webb rung, predating the binding-layer adjudication), and that convolution is the interval a reader who wants one number for sampling error should use. I do not substitute it for the floor read, because independence between a 2018 cohort-month ratio and a window-period composition resample is an assumption rather than a measurement. The hull's lower edge is thus narrower than the layer the paper calls binding; the two must be quoted together, not the hull alone.

\subsubsection{The floor's provenance and the off-window re-anchor}

The floor's provenance now has an out-of-agency read as well as an out-of-window one, and it does not narrow the range. The committed off-window rule, applied unchanged to the Fannie Mae cohort-month panel, returns 5.52\% on the headline cell against Freddie's 5.19\%, on 421 cohort-months against 438 (run \texttt{fannie\_floor\_read}). That panel carries the same fourteen columns and the same 2017 start, and it was staged for the external replication of Section~\ref{sec:pathb-fannie}. The Freddie panel replays every committed cell of that grid through the identical code path first. The Fannie read clears the clean band's 5.334\% top, but it is no second read of the clean leg. Off window the rule covers pooled 2017--2019, whose Freddie counterpart is that 5.19\%, not the 2018 leg's 4.991\%. So the agency difference is 0.337 points of CPR (5.522\% against 5.185\%), and the rest is period. The age-standardized 5.51\% lands 0.01 points away, but it perturbs something else: an age correction to the 2018 leg itself, on 84.0\% imputed weight. The near-coincidence of the two is therefore not a second measurement of either. What they share is direction---both above the clean top, both saying the clean read understates the mature book's turnover. That is what the clean leg cannot test on its own. The 2018 leg holds no out-of-the-money cohort-month past the twenty-four-month age cut, so its mature-seasoning test is not computable (Appendix~\ref{app:floormech}). A test that cannot be run has not been passed. The one age contrast the leg does support is confounded by the PSA ramp. Where a mature contrast is measurable at all (two agencies, two rate regimes, three depth cuts), it runs one way in twelve cells of twelve, the age-$\geq$-24 read the higher one in each. In window that read is higher by 0.08 and 0.19 points of CPR on Freddie and Fannie, the rate configuration suppressing refinancing as on the 2018 leg. On the refi-contaminated pooled 2017--2019 period it is higher by 3.74 and 4.61 points, which bounds the correction from above without measuring it. A floor read confined to cohort-months aged twelve to twenty-four therefore understates mature baseline turnover and overstates the marginal. The error is signed against the headline; its size is not priced anywhere in this paper. On the committed floor-to-marginal grid a 5.52\% floor brackets the marginal between the 5.334\% row's $+4.27$ points and the 6.0\% row's $+2.28$, below the $+4.3$ lower edge this paper quotes. I do not restate the headline on the Fannie floor: the production calibration is the Freddie one, and the two books differ in composition as well as in this read. So the out-of-agency read establishes only that the band's \emph{lower} edge is the soft one, the direction the age-standardized caveat already pointed.

That sensitivity makes the floor's provenance decisive. So Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor} re-measures the floor outside the evaluation window and re-runs both legs there. The marginal lands at $+4.3$ to $+6.8$ points across the defensible off-window range---inside the box, well clear of either edge---and stays positive at all twenty-seven floor-by-band cells run. The covariate-prior swing of Section~\ref{sec:pathb} is the design's largest single level sensitivity at both calibrations. At the headline floor it exceeds the marginal: re-run at the 4.991\% floor, the two covariate endpoints span 8.2 points of recovery against the $+5.6$-point marginal. The marginal is the counter-fact. Switching the covariate block between its endpoints moves the off-window marginal by only $+0.1$ points, against $+1.9$ in-window, because both legs shift together where the floor censors more.

\subsubsection{The sign check}

The marginal's positivity in all twenty-seven cells is not the robust identified content. Over the forty window months the cohort panel covers (June 2022 through September 2025; the panel ends two months before the window does), the exposure-weighted mean coupon on the surviving book is 3.134\%, with 97.40\% of exposure at or below 4.79\% and 99.53\% at or below 5.09\%. Set those figures against a window-minimum 30-year rate of 5.2311\%. That minimum is the August 2022 monthly mean --- the window's rate dip --- not a June 2022 figure (\texttt{sign\_forcing\_stats}). The two uncovered months cannot overturn it: the book is closed at 2017--2021 originations, so no higher-coupon loan can enter, and the out-of-the-money share can only hold or rise as low-coupon cohorts season. Essentially the entire book is out of the money for essentially the entire window, so the rate gap is negative in every cell of every sweep reported here. A specification in which a more negative gap suppresses prepayment cannot then return a negative marginal at any floor or band in the box. The sign is fixed by the rate configuration, not by the estimated elasticity. What the twenty-seven-cell check verifies is accordingly the specification: it would catch a wiring error that flipped a coefficient or crossed the central and null legs, and I retain it on that footing. This is why the reported range, and not the sign, carries the paper's identified content. The band's monotone response is forced the same way: \eqref{eq:beta1} is monotone in $\delta$ and the hazard monotone in $\beta_1$ at that gap, so the ordering composes monotone maps. That ordering I retain as a wiring check on the same footing, not as identified content; the sweep's endpoints stay the convention range they are.

\subsubsection{What the design does not claim}

A second qualification: timing carries no credential. The $\beta_1 = 0$ null also peaks at lag $-3$ (Section~\ref{sec:hazard-interp}), so the lead cannot discriminate the elasticity from the rate-inelastic model. A third qualification: the marginal's sampling precision is not the operative uncertainty. Its premise is now the cluster scheme's, not the retracted one's. At this calibration the stratum-cluster bootstrap puts the paired marginal at $[+8.27, +10.19]$ points, while the within-stratum $[+9.17, +9.23]$ understates by 30.8$\times$ (Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). The calibration box, 11.1 points wide against the cluster read's 1.9, still governs the interval.

A fourth qualification concerns composition. The decomposition exhibits in this subsection, computed at the in-sample calibration point, partition the $+\$70.3$ billion figure and report shares and intensities. The floor's level shifts those quantities far less than it shifts the total. Group ablation (zeroing $\beta_1$ cell by cell, everything else at production) shows the $+\$70.3$ billion broad-based, not cohort-specific. All fifteen vintage $\times$ coupon cells are positive, they sum to within 1.03\% of the paired-run total, and no cell exceeds 21\%. The 2020--2021 vintages carry 72\% of the marginal on 73\% of balance, and per-balance intensity runs from $0.82\times$ to $1.75\times$. On the dimensions that grid misses --- FICO ($<$680 / 680--740 / 740$+$), LTV ($\leq$80 / $>$80), Census region --- the same engine returns broad-based on all three. Every cell in those partitions is positive too. The sums reproduce the $+\$70.3$ billion total within interaction residuals of 0.8, 0.4, and 0.5 percent, and per-balance intensity across the three lies in 0.88--1.26, tighter than the vintage $\times$ coupon span. That verdict does not, on its own, discriminate, and I say so. Twelve random three-way partitions of the same 75,000 loans, run through the identical engine and pre-committed rule, return \emph{broad-based} in all twelve---on intensities of 0.995--1.007 and additivity residuals of 0.49 percent (\texttt{subgroup\_marginals\_placebo}). A partition with no economic content earns the label, so the label alone carries no evidential weight. On the benchmark basis, the 0.2-point proximity of the projection's uniform-spread 88.5\% upper end to the null's in-sample 88.7\% recovery is a doubly conditional coincidence, not corroboration. Section~\ref{sec:method-benchmark} withdraws that presentation, states both conditions with their magnitudes, and reports projection range and null separately. I claim no agreement from their proximity. Every percentage-point magnitude quoted for this decomposition is cap-basis, while dollars and rankings are basis-invariant.

\paragraph{Distributional incidence.} The placebo strips the homogeneity verdict of its evidential content, but a distributional finding survives it: who bears the mobility cost, not whether a label discriminates. The comparison establishes the reverse: the random partitions' intensities concentrate on unity, the widest of the twelve spans 0.011, while the economic groupings span 0.381, more than thirty times wider. The informative statement is therefore not homogeneity, which chance alone delivers, but dispersion well beyond chance: the tilts are structure, not sampling noise, and the generality claim rests on sign homogeneity and the bounded magnitude of that dispersion, not on the verdict label. Those tilts run mildly toward below-740-FICO ($1.23\times$--$1.26\times$) and above-80-LTV ($1.23\times$) borrowers, regional intensities spanning only 0.93--1.06 (Figure~\ref{fig:marginalcells}). The marginal is a book-wide property of the rate-gap distribution interacting with seasoning and the floor, so the lock-in cost falls mildly more than proportionally on weaker-credit and higher-leverage borrowers, close to evenly across regions --- a statement about where a book-wide effect concentrates, measured against a placebo that fixes what chance alone would deliver; it is not evidence of a subgroup-specific mechanism, and this design does not carry it to household welfare.

The monthly-path and coupon-bucket timing exhibits are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:patha-detail}. Neither simulated leg carries calendar-month seasonality, so seasonal structure enters these exhibits only through the empirical comparison. Per the pre-committed posture of Section~\ref{sec:hazard-interp}, they are fit diagnostics, not timing credentials.

A fifth qualification states what kind of claim ``identifies'' makes. The marginal is a within-model counterfactual decomposition: the difference between two runs of one fully disclosed hazard structure, differing only in the elasticity term. It is not causal identification in the econometric sense. This paper exploits no quasi-experimental variation in rate gaps across otherwise-comparable loans, and it claims none. The causal content of $\beta_1$ is inherited, not asserted. I import the elasticity unmodified from \citepos{liebersohn2024} estimated band, with \citet{fonseca2024} and the transaction-record band corroboration of \citet{graybill2026} as independent support. No Path B parameter is estimated on the benchmark (Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). The switch-off adds accounting at a stated price: the marginal inherits any misspecification of the held-fixed structure. So I sweep the channels I can name --- floor level and functional form, the rate-gap transform, the covariate priors --- each directly (Sections~\ref{sec:pathb} and \ref{sec:robustness-floor}). Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty} reports calibration, not sampling, as the operative uncertainty.

A sixth qualification concerns three readings I decline in three separate places. Path A's fitted rate-gap coefficient is $+0.29$ per 100 bp against the imported $\beta_1$'s $0.069$, on different scales. I therefore compare the two on sign not magnitude (Section~\ref{sec:patha}). The realized cross-cohort gap gradient is roughly $4.5\times$ what the central $\beta_1$ implies. The real-covariate cross-design variant recovers 59.3\% of the benchmark, 76.3\% reweighted to the book's composition, against the production ABM's 13.6\% seed mean (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign}). I stand by each reason. The first is not unit-comparable and does not survive a bias-respecting sign test. By contrast, the second survives composition standardization ($+3.44$ against the implied $+0.94$, run \texttt{episode\_confrontation\_within}). I decline it on the narrower ground that remains. Within-window refinancing on shallow buckets is inseparable from moving, and a level gradient is not a dollar marginal. Finally, the third is a recovery level, which this design does not identify, and the same reweight moves the frozen cross-design variant down to 12.6\%. Stated in one place: none of the three points toward a smaller lock-in channel. I keep the imported value for Section~\ref{sec:pathb}'s reason. Path B is a structural verification exercise whose elasticity is estimated in a different setting and never touches this benchmark. Re-fitting it in-sample would end the exercise. Among available literature estimates the adopted band is already the conservative one. \citepos{fonseca2024} anchor puts the marginal at $+11.5$ points against the central calibration's $+9.2$ at the production floor. Other disclosed variants move it the other way, the Ginnie-speed overlay to $+4.4$ points and the age-standardized floor read to a measured $+3.7$ (Table~\ref{tab:uncertainty}). So I do not read the three as a case for a larger headline.

\subsubsection{The assembled corrections}

A seventh qualification assembles those readings against the ones that run the other way, because this paper states each correction where it arises and never lines them up. Lined up they show what the sections do not. Holding the production hard-maximum form and the imported elasticity fixed, every correction to the floor or to the accounting basis moves the headline down; re-solved under the additive form, the calibrated companion below moves it up instead. The in-sample calibration returns $+9.2$ points. Re-anchoring the floor outside the window returns the headline $+5.6$. The Ginnie-speed overlay at that same floor returns $+4.4$. The age-standardized floor read of 5.51\% returns a measured marginal of $+3.7$, against the $+3.8$ its position on the frozen grid implies. The Fannie Mae read of the same rule on the pooled off-window period, 5.52\%, brackets the marginal below the $+4.3$ edge of the clean band. Its like-for-like Freddie counterpart is 5.19\%, not the 2018 leg's 4.991\%. The anchor convention does not flatter the headline. The middle of the five reads the 10\% support rule admits is 4.869\%, below the adopted 4.991\%, and a lower floor implies a marginal above the headline. Carrying the overlay's $0.797$ scaling up to the age-standardized floor projected a marginal near $+3.0$. The joint cell has now been run under a pre-committed landing rule (run \texttt{b5\_joint\_cell}) and measures $+2.9$ points, an interaction within $0.1$ of proportional. By contrast, this paper's first joint cell returned an interaction of $-1.47$ points (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}). Corrections do not compose additively, and a composed figure would overstate precision. Nor are they independent: floor corrections and the censoring share are one mechanism read twice, since raising the floor crowds the elasticity out. At the 4.991\% headline floor the central leg is already pinned to the floor in 68.8\% of evaluated loan-months, against 36.3\% at the in-sample calibration. Further increases have progressively less hazard left to act on. That share is no claim that the estimate rests on a third of the data. A pinned month's contribution is truncated at the floor rather than erased. The $\beta_1 = 0$ leg is pinned in only 35.8\% of loan-months there, so the legs separate wherever the null clears it. These are unweighted loan-month counts rather than balance-weighted shares, so nothing follows about the uncensored months' share of the dollar marginal. What the assembly settles is where in the interval the mass sits, not whether the finding holds. Within the production form the widest disclosed layer is the baseline level. Sweeping the seasoning ramp over 75 to 150 PSA on both legs carries the marginal from $+0.9$ to $+15.6$ points at the off-window floor and $+3.3$ to $+16.5$ at the in-sample calibration. That span has no coverage property: it is a range over a convention I did not estimate, its endpoints not draws from anything. Aggregate fit is no defense of the 100~PSA convention. At the off-window floor the $\beta_1 = 0$ null recovers 101.9\% of the benchmark under the standalone scorer at 75~PSA against 94.8\% at the production 100, while the central leg is closer at 100 (100.4\%) than at 75 --- the legs rank the ramps opposite ways. A conditional estimate of the ramp's \emph{shape} now exists and does not favour the headline. Transporting the age profile of Path~A's estimated seasoning spline onto $h_0$ returns a median marginal of $+9.44$ points, 5th-to-95th-percentile spread $+8.99$ to $+9.81$, across the 107 of 200 bootstrap replicates whose normalised profile stays inside the usable range (run \texttt{h0\_reanchor}). The level is held fixed by construction, absorbed by the fitted constant and stratum fixed effects and not separately identified. Every replicate lies above the $+5.6$ this paper headlines and all but one above the $+8.7$ upper edge of the binding interval quoted below, though all lie inside the $75$--$150$~PSA span. With 46.5\% discarded it is a conditional estimate, not the ramp's sampling interval, so the convention span stays labelled unestimated. I had pre-committed to the opposite sign: that re-anchoring would push more loan-months onto the floor and so \emph{lower} the marginal. The bind share did rise as predicted, from 35.8\% to between 50.4\% and 58.2\% on the null leg, and the marginal rose anyway: a failed prediction, reported as one. The floor read itself transports in two directions, both fixed before either ran (run \texttt{floor\_transport\_two\_sided}). Standardizing its calendar raises it: the 2018 leg is read in August--December---two July cohort-months aside---on the declining side of the committed seasonal profile. Re-weighting to the window's calendar mix carries 4.991\% to 5.2156\% and the marginal to $+4.70$ points. Matching its housing-activity level lowers it: active listings averaged 1.27 million over those 2018 months against 0.80 million over the window. The same multiplicative transport carries the floor to 3.1533\% and the marginal to $+10.69$. I report the two at their own floors and do not net them: two floor-side adjustments composed with an interaction of $-1.47$ points the one time this paper ran them jointly. A cancellation would be a finding requiring its own cell, not arithmetic. The activity leg is a listings-based proxy for an argument about existing-home \emph{sales}, not constructible here (the sales series carries no observations over the 2018 leg months), and its magnitude inherits a one-for-one activity-to-turnover mapping nothing here estimates. The sign is what it establishes. Post-stratification onto the book's composition moves the marginal less than the paper's own share arithmetic implies. Raking the pool's balance weights to the SOMA coupon marginal and the book's vintage mix within the draw's 2017--2021 support (both margins hit to better than $10^{-10}$) returns $+5.11$ points against the unweighted $+5.57$, a move of $-0.47$. The vintage margin alone returns $+4.67$, so the coupon margin offsets about half the vintage correction. The vintage overlay's $+3.7$, pure share scaling, overstates the composition correction roughly fourfold. Two limits travel with it. The book's coupon-by-vintage \emph{joint} distribution is not observable (the SOMA cohort tabulation collapses vintage to one value-weighted origination date per coupon bucket), so this matches two margins, not a cell grid. The pre-2017 and 2022 vintages, 33.7\% of book face, have no draw support, are not re-weighted here, and stay with the vintage overlay. I pre-committed both a direction and a band: the direction held and the marginal fell, while $+5.11$ lies just outside the $[+2.5, +5.0]$ band. I report that miss. I do not re-draw the band. The widest layer that does have a coverage property is the floor reads' own sampling error, $+2.3$ to $+9.1$ points after wild-cluster correction of the percentile read's under-coverage (run \texttt{floor\_inference\_correction}; the percentile read is $+3.0$ to $+8.0$). That is the interval this paper quotes as binding. The headline sits just below its midpoint, every correction listed above falls in its lower half, and the uncomposed pair near its bottom. Resolution should be read off those magnitudes, not the printed decimals. Against a binding interval roughly seven points wide the weighting scheme moves an endpoint about $0.06$ of a point, so the interval is credible to something like half a point and one-decimal endpoint comparisons fall below it. The two are nested: the ramp sweep is the outer statement, $+2.3$ to $+9.1$ a sampling interval on the floor read alone, nested inside it at the production 100~PSA ramp. Which rung of that inner layer to quote is settled by the pre-committed coverage rule of run \texttt{fewcluster\_coverage}, not by width. The restricted inversion is the narrowest construction clearing the $93.0\%/91.0\%$ bar, and CR2 at Bell--McCaffrey degrees of freedom ($+2.4$ to $+9.1$ points), the other qualifying rung, stands beside it, not behind. A designed coverage simulation at this read's committed leverage profile (run \texttt{fewcluster\_coverage}: 31 clusters, 5.9 effective, maximum leverage 0.33; 5{,}000 draws $\times$ 999 replications) covers 94.3\% under Gaussian and 95.1\% under $t_5$ cluster disturbances for the restricted wild-cluster inversion. The comparators are 90.8\%/91.4\% for the Webb wild-$t$, below the spec's pre-committed $93.0\%/91.0\%$ qualification bar, 84.0\%/85.7\% for CR1, 89.5\%/89.9\% for the demoted percentile read, and 99.5\%/99.6\% (over-covering, and wider) for CR2 at Bell--McCaffrey. The frozen selection rule therefore lands the binding layer on the restricted inversion, whose $[+2.3, +9.1]$ this paper quotes. The Webb wild-$t$'s narrower $[+2.9, +8.7]$ is reported beside it, no longer as the binding layer. So the binding-layer choice is measured, not asserted, and the rule was applied after seeing which way it fell. What that layer prices is itself form-conditional: the same $0.99$-point rise in the floor that carries the read off window moves the marginal by $-3.63$ points under the production form and by $-0.04$ under the additive one. The interval's width is as much a property of the max form's censoring as of the floor read. Two further v20 companions price adjacent layers. Re-run on each leg's own counterfactual balance path, not renormalized monthly to realized holdings (run \texttt{compounding\_consistent\_null}), the marginal is $+4.84$ points (\$37.0 billion; central 92.3\%, null 87.4\% shared). That prices the renormalization's disclosed upper-bound bias at $0.74$ points within the quoted interval. The floor read's sampling layer propagated to the null's level puts a wild-cluster interval of $[80.6, 88.4]$\% on the 85.7\%, beside the floor-range band of Table~\ref{tab:headline}. I attach no posture to where $+5.6$ sits among these readings. With the downward variants lined up beside the upward counterweights, no interior member is privileged, and the range rather than any point is what the design delivers. The form-conditional hull of $+3.5$ to $+13.1$ points remains the wider statement, since the form dimension is not a floor correction and is not disciplined by this list. Three things stop the list from becoming a retraction. The sign is fixed by the window's rate configuration, not by these calibrations, as the sign-forcing argument above establishes, so nothing here bears on the direction. Zero-exclusion is deliberately not among the three, a forced sign making it a restatement of that configuration. Specification choices that are not floor corrections run both ways, and this assembly lines both up. Upward: the additive form returns $+11.2$ points at the same off-window anchors, nearly floor-invariantly, and sets the hull's upper end. \citepos{fonseca2024} anchor would put the marginal at $+11.5$ points at the production floor against the adopted band's $+9.2$, and the housing-activity calibration below, re-solved under that form, joins them. Downward: the concave transform alone returns $+5.06$ at the off-window point floor against the production $+5.57$, and the sub-band elasticity at $\delta = 3.25\%$ returns $+3.47$ (Table~\ref{tab:lowband}). The moving-share bracket at the headline floor returns $+3.36$ points at $\mu = 0.5$ and $+1.82$ at $\mu = 0.25$ against $+5.57$ at $\mu = 1$. It is a convention bracket, not an estimate, mildly super-proportional because censored contributions do not scale with $\mu$. The survival-selection attenuation of the same imported coefficient is a bracket of the same kind and belongs here: $\eta = S_{\mathrm{pre}}^{\xi}$ at the measured pre-window survival share carries the marginal from $+5.1$ at $\xi = 0.25$ to $+3.7$ at $\xi = 1$, $\xi$ free (Section~\ref{sec:pathb}) --- a different axis from the elasticity band, not a widening of it. The assembly is not exhaustive over runs scoring a marginal at a labelled floor. The PSA sweep, the covariate ablation and the floor-form mixture do so too, reported at their own sites. I state no rule admitting these rows while excluding those. A calibrated companion prices the omitted housing-activity level term (Table~\ref{tab:specbox}) from the paper's own catalogued anchor. \citet{aladangady2024} attribute 44\% of the 2021--2022 mobility decline to rate-gap lock-in. Scaling the seasoning baseline in both legs until the non-rate channel traps $0.56/0.44$ times the rate channel's trapped liquidity puts the headline floor's root at $\phi^{*} = 0.754$ (effectively 75.4 PSA) and the marginal at $+0.9$ points at the headline floor and $+3.5$ at the in-sample calibration (run \texttt{scaled\_null\_housing\_activity}). The $\phi = 1$ cells reproduce the committed values bit-exactly. The variant documents an upward bias under both forms; under the production form the corrected member lies below the headline, at the bottom of the ramp span just swept, and at the headline floor falls outside the quoted $+2.3$ to $+9.1$ interval. That is an externally anchored calibration the quoted interval does not cover, not a challenge to the sign. At the in-sample calibration it falls inside. Re-solving the same condition under the additive form barely moves the root ($\phi^{*} = 0.756$ against $0.754$ at the headline floor, a direction I declined to predict). It lands the surviving marginal at $+8.5$ points at both anchors, clearing the $+4.0$-point bar fixed before the run (run \texttt{scaled\_null\_housing\_activity\_additive}; the $\phi = 1$ cells reproduce the committed additive legs bit-exactly). The calibration cuts the additive form's own $+11.2$ to $+8.5$ as it cuts the production form's $+5.6$ to $+0.9$. What the form decides is where the corrected member lands: above the headline at both anchors and near the top of the quoted interval under the additive form, below the headline at both under the production one. Censoring separates them: at the production form's root the central leg is pinned to the floor in 94.4\% of evaluated loan-months and the maximum truncates the elasticity there. The additive form combines the two hazards on the survival scale, so the floor, which dominates no less often at the additive root, truncates nothing. On that side of the fork this reading belongs with the counterweights above rather than with the ladder. That the floor side runs one way is also an absence. The floor is a level of total turnover, not of a strictly involuntary component (Section~\ref{sec:pathb}), so it carries its period's activity. Re-reading it at the window's own level enters at the floor, not the ramp just scaled, and so cuts the other way --- a lower anchor raises the marginal. It is the one floor-side correction I can name that would move the headline up, turning on whether the window's turnover level is the lower of the two, which I do not measure here. And the three readings of the preceding paragraph point to a larger lock-in channel, not a smaller one. I decline those three for the reasons given there and decline to compose these for the reason given here: the identified content is the range. Floor and basis corrections concentrate in its lower half, and the form-side counterweights sit at its top or above it. Table~\ref{tab:assembly} tabulates this assembly.
