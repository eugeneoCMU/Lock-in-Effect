\section{Agent-Based Model Results: A Synthetic-Population Stress Test}\label{sec:abm}

This section is a stress test of the household-choice hypothesis, not the paper's main estimate, and its cross-design leg fixes the scope of everything below. Re-running the same decision rule on real Freddie Mac structural covariates recovers 59.3\% of the benchmark on the committed draw and 60.2\% averaged over fifty seeds---above the 50\% threshold fixed before that run at which this section's paradigm reading would be undercut. It is undercut on all fifty. That leg runs under the recalibration rule the model already applies elsewhere, and recovery rises to 76.3\% once the sample is reweighted to the SOMA book's own coupon composition. Three things bound that reading wherever it appears. First, the same reweight moves the frozen-calibration leg the other way, down to 12.6\%. Second, the covariates swapped in are structural only---the loan records contain no analogue for mobility desire or loss aversion---so no leg here replaces a synthetic behavioral population with a real one. Third, the verdict the run supports is that the contrast cannot, on that test alone, be cleanly attributed to modeling paradigm rather than to calibration and data source (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign}). What this section falsifies is therefore a \emph{synthetic-population} household-choice specification: no specification tested here explains the Federal Reserve's cap shortfall, and why it does not is not cleanly separable from the population it was tested on. A second qualifier travels with the verdict: the multi-vintage stage moved against its own predicted direction, and the inversion is undiagnosed (Appendix~\ref{sec:robustness-extensions}). The paradigm reading this section was written to advance is withdrawn on the strength of that crossing; Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign} records the withdrawal where the test lives.

Section~\ref{sec:robustness}'s scale-sensitivity replication finds no size effect on the production specification. Two possibilities remain: either household-level decision-making does not govern the majority of the shortfall, or this specification is missing a mechanism. The cross-design leg bears on that either-or without settling it: the same rule's 60.2\% on real structural covariates points at the population rather than the mechanism, but the behavioral layer stays synthetic in both legs, so the alternative is narrowed, not eliminated. Section~\ref{sec:identification} \emph{declines} that same recovery as evidence that the imported lock-in elasticity is too small, since a recovery level is not what that design identifies; used here only as a scope limit, it survives that objection, and neither use licenses the other. The clean falsification bypasses that free parameter entirely: the simulated-minimum-distance two-moment fit returns joint infeasibility under a verdict vocabulary fixed before execution, its nearest admissible fit overshoots the benchmark at 107.1\%, and it does not run through the recalibration scale that qualifies the cross-design reading (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-crossdesign}). Section~\ref{sec:hazard} tests the first branch directly, on a different data source and modeling paradigm. Whichever explanation holds, these results weigh against household mobility choices, rational or behavioral, as the primary price the Federal Reserve paid for secondary-market liquidity. The institutional counterpart is the securitization-policy alternative carried as appendix material (Section~\ref{sec:abm-danish}): whether the par-payoff rule itself, not household behavior, drives the trap.

\subsection{The Synthetic-Population Estimate: Baseline, Production, Extensions, and the Burnout Falsification}\label{sec:abm-results}

Against the \$764.7 billion benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}), a purely rational baseline projects a structural cash flow deficit of \$544.0 billion (71.1\% of benchmark). The production specification adds scheduled amortization, a live CUSIP-level SOMA parse, a corrected empirical CPR back-out, a settlement-lag kernel, and the previously excluded 15-year MBS book in cohort weighting and scheduled amortization (structural terms only). Its headline quantity is the seed distribution, not any single draw: the fifty-seed mean is \$103.7 billion, 13.6\% of the benchmark, and the frozen seed-42 draw (\$91.0 billion, 11.9\%, near the 34th percentile) is the reproducibility anchor the run manifests freeze. The seed-level standard deviation is \$24.5 billion ($\pm$1~SD share band 10.4--16.8\%), the 95\% CI of the mean, \$96.9--\$110.5 billion, prices only simulation noise in the mean itself, and the 34th percentile is pinned only to within roughly $\pm$7 percentage points from 50 draws. The ABM's share is therefore better read as on the order of 10--17\% than as a three-digit point estimate (Figure~\ref{fig:mc}). The kernel is a documented timing null, not a fit improvement---a pre-fold-in ablation left the peak cross-correlation unchanged at $r = +0.192$ at lag $-3$ with the kernel on or off, and the kernel-applied production run, which every figure here reports, peaks at $+0.195$ at that lag---but it is retained in production and recorded as applied in both frozen run manifests. The path fit is worse than the level fit and no extension repairs it (Figure~\ref{fig:abmpaths}): simulated mean CPR is 11.68\% against an empirical 5.14\% (ABM basis), the raw path correlation is negative at lag 0 ($r = -0.318$), nothing in a $\pm$3-month window exceeds the $+0.19$ at the three-month lead, and the path $R^2$ is $-6.984$, where $R^2 = 1-\mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}$ against a mean-only null throughout, so a negative value is a fit worse than a constant at the empirical mean. The claimed object is the aggregate level, and the path statistics are a disclosed failure, not a fitted quantity: no level result in this paper is scored on path fit, and no estimator reproduces the monthly path once the window's shared trend is removed (Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). Three literature-grounded behavioral extensions each move the model further from the benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:waterfall}), the first a recalibration artifact rather than evidence for loss aversion: a real-world turnover floor held fixed under a steeper loss-aversion penalty mechanically inflates rate sensitivity. None touches the CPR path's central failure; each acts on the level of simulated mobility, not its timing. A fourth variant fails more informatively. Path-dependent prepayment ``burnout,'' implemented as survivor selection in a closed population of 10,000 agents, collapsed simulated CPR to approximately zero and raised trapped liquidity to 147\% of the benchmark, because that population depletes its mobile mass in the low-rate 2020--2021 window before QT begins. That is an absorbing state incompatible with a real MBS pool that continuously receives new originations, and \citepos{lesniewski2026} selection identity in its degenerate limit. It is a negative finding, not used in production, and why burnout enters Section~\ref{sec:hazard} at the cohort level. Appendices~\ref{sec:method-abm}, \ref{sec:method-frictions}, \ref{app:abmspec}, \ref{sec:robustness-extensions} and~\ref{sec:robustness-pipeline} specify the decision rule, every behavioral parameter and its source, the friction and structural extensions, the behavioral stages' mechanisms, and the 15-year fold-in; the replication package's run ledger reproduces them end to end.

One asymmetry deserves statement. Section~\ref{sec:identification} insists that this design identifies a differential and not a level, yet I compare the ABM with Path B on levels, because the ABM admits no counterpart to the $\beta_1 = 0$ null. In \eqref{eq:pathB} the elasticity is a separable term over an independently measured floor that survives the elasticity's removal, so the null stays anchored to observed baseline turnover and lands at 85.7\% of the benchmark. The ABM has no such term. Its 4--5\% turnover floor is not a component of \eqref{eq:abmmove} but a \emph{product} of it, found by searching the mobility-desire scale $\theta$ until the penalty-bearing rule returns 4--5\% CPR at an 8\% market rate, so zeroing the penalty removes the floor along with the channel. With the production $\theta = 43{,}882.8$ held fixed, the resulting leg returns 40.7\% CPR at the calibration point against the production rule's 4.8\%; it over-retires the book and scores $-259.4$\% of the benchmark. Re-deriving $\theta$ under the null restores the anchor to 4.7\% but scores $+116.3$\%, because the re-search fits the null to the very turnover level the differential is meant to measure. The two readings imply marginals of $+270.4$ and $-105.3$ points: they do not bracket a magnitude; they disagree in sign, and neither is interpretable. I therefore report no ABM differential, and the estimator contrast of Section~\ref{sec:hazard} stays a contrast of levels, with the qualification that entails (run \texttt{abm\_null\_feasibility}; a feasibility probe, not a pre-committed estimate, and nothing in this paper's ranges depends on it).
