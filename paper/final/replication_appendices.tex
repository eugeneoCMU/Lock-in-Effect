\section{Superseded Figures and Run Ledger}\label{app:ledger}

\paragraph{Data and code availability.} The replication package is the public repository at \url{https://github.com/eugeneoCMU/Lock-in-Effect}. It ships all estimation, simulation, and figure code; every committed results artifact this paper cites (the results JSONs, the production coefficient artifact, the exposure-weighted delinquency-transition matrix); the tagged run manifests with SHA-256 pins on their artifact files; and the machine-checked liveness gates and the claims-versus-code audit artifact. Hazards are estimated on the Freddie Mac Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset, which Freddie Mac makes publicly available at no cost after registration. Freddie inputs are code-only, on the same conservative footing as the Fannie inputs below: neither the raw origination and performance files nor the derived 75{,}000-loan sample and cohort-month panel are redistributed here. Freddie Mac's terms permit publishing derived products only where they cannot be used to derive or recreate any part of the dataset, and a loan-grain sample does not clearly meet that condition (nor does a cohort-month panel whose smallest cells hold a single loan), so I do not ship either and make no claim about where the line falls. The committed code rebuilds every derived object from a user's own download under fixed seeds (engine seed 42 with per-regime offsets; every post-freeze run's specification is fixed ex ante in its script header, the convention this ledger records). Macro and portfolio inputs are the public FRED and New York Fed SOMA interfaces; a user-supplied FRED key is the only credential involved. Fannie Mae inputs are code-only, as Section~\ref{sec:pathb-fannie} states: no Fannie data and no derived cells ship, and the committed replication artifact carries headline statistics alone.

Headline figures in this paper changed across drafts as specification errors were found and corrected. Because some superseded values appear in earlier internal drafts and in the accompanying technical documentation, I catalogue them here rather than in scattered footnotes, so that a reader consulting both always knows which figure superseded which, and why.

\begingroup\sloppy
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{U.S. CPR 12.3\%, empirical CPR 5.45\%, wedge 35.1pp}: produced by a window filter that inadvertently included eight post-QT months; superseded by the bounded active-QT window (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-benchmark}).
\item \textbf{17.6pp wedge; 21--27\% Danish CPR}: a pre-multi-cohort, rational-only calibration; superseded by the multi-vintage production specification.
\item \textbf{\$101.2 billion / 13.2\% ABM estimate} (\texttt{run-2026-07-04}): the pre-fold-in, 30-year-only tag, raw path $R^2 = -6.443$; superseded by the 15-year fold-in production tag \texttt{run-2026-07-04-15yr-foldin} (\$91.0 billion / 11.9\%, $R^2 = -6.984$; Appendix~\ref{sec:robustness-pipeline}).
\item \textbf{Danish CPR 47.1\%; wedge $\approx$35.5pp; institutional gap \$925.5 billion}: the mechanism-extrapolated Danish specification; the 35.5pp wedge is the difference between its 47.1\% Danish and 11.68\% U.S. mean CPR. Superseded by the Berger-recalibrated \texttt{run-2026-07-05-berger} (Danish CPR $\approx$3.4\%; institutional gap $-\$728.4$ billion against that run's own \$84.5 billion U.S. leg; Section~\ref{sec:abm-danish}).
\item \textbf{``recovers 97.9\%, within 2.1\% of the benchmark'' as an unqualified headline}: the recovery level and the lock-in marginal were quoted at different turnover-floor calibrations after the marginal was demoted to the off-window floor. Both are retained, each labelled with its floor: 91.3\% recovery and an 8.7-point miss at the headline off-window floor (88.2--93.7\% across its range), 97.9\% and 2.1 points at the in-sample calibration point (\texttt{calibration\_reconciliation}). The related 0.2-point agreement between the null's 88.7\% and the Fed's 88.5\% ex-ante projection share is retracted as a corroborating coincidence: it requires both the in-sample floor and the uniform-spread allocation.

\item \textbf{18.3-point real-book central-minus-null gap}: differenced a standalone-scorer central leg against a shared-basis null. The single-basis gap is 9.2 points in-sample and 5.6 points at the off-window floor.

\item \textbf{Path B \$747 billion / 97.7\%}: the pre-unit-correction microsimulation, in which the calibrated lock-in elasticity was numerically inert; superseded by \$818.5 billion / 107.0\% (Appendix~\ref{sec:robustness-pipeline}). Its corrected analogue, the deliberate $\beta_1 = 0$ null of Section~\ref{sec:hazard-interp}, recovers 97.8\%.
\item \textbf{Path A \$915.0 billion / 119.7\%} (spec v3; $r = -0.444$ at lag 0, peak $-3$): the prior production specification, which omitted calendar-month seasonality; superseded by the calendar-month spec v4 adopted at the pre-submission freeze on its timing behaviour, the peak moving to $-2$; the recovery difference between the two specifications is not calendar-attributable and no part of the adoption case rests on it (\$928.9 billion / 121.5\%; \texttt{run-2026-07-14-pathA-seasonal}; Section~\ref{sec:patha}).
\item \textbf{Hybrid Danish gap $-\$99.9$ billion, ``sign not robustly identified''} (Danish-level anchor; Danish mean CPR 3.39\%): superseded as the production reading by the U.S.-intercept transplant (\texttt{danish\_us\_intercept}: gap $+\$61.2$ billion, Danish mean CPR 5.61\%, positive at every refinance-in-place sweep point, that positivity being forced by the zero-gap anchor; the U.S. leg reproduces the committed central artifacts exactly). The Danish-level rows are retained in Table~\ref{tab:danish} as a labeled rule-plus-country bracketing case; their Danish mean CPRs sit below the 4\% turnover floor, the inconsistency that motivated the restatement (Section~\ref{sec:abm-danish}).
\item \textbf{Post-freeze robustness artifacts}: the floor functional-form test (\texttt{hazard/floor\_form\_results.json}: additive-form marginal $+11.25$pp at the production floor, $+11.29$ to $+11.21$ across 3--5\% floors; max-form parity pair reproduces the committed artifacts exactly; Section~\ref{sec:robustness-floor}) and the Path B stratified loan-level bootstrap (\texttt{hazard/bootstrap\_pathb\_results.json}; Section~\ref{sec:pathb}). Neither changes a production headline; both are specified ex ante in their script headers, written before the runs executed. The seasonal-floor run follows the same convention (\texttt{hazard/seasonal\_floor\_timing\_results.json} and the placebo scoring in \texttt{hazard/data/b3\_timing\_scores.json}; Appendix~\ref{sec:robustness-seasonalfloor}), and carries one correction to its own provenance: an earlier module docstring described the twelve-cell calendar profile as estimated in-window, when it reproduces on the full cohort-month panel. Both bases are recorded in the artifact, the full-panel basis drives every run, and no figure elsewhere in this paper consumed the profile.
\item \textbf{``log-normal mobility-desire heterogeneity'' (scale-sensitivity description)}: the production ABM draws mobility desire from an exponential distribution on a dollar scale; the log-normal description was an error of description, not of code, caught by an adversarial claims-versus-code check and corrected in place (Section~\ref{sec:robustness-scale}).
\end{itemize}
\endgroup

Every current headline figure cites a frozen, tagged run manifest; two freezes ran on uncommitted working trees, so manifest git hashes alone do not name an exact reproduction checkout, and the repository's run ledger records the exact reproduction commits. The one that carries a headline quantity should be named here rather than left to the ledger: the fold-in Monte Carlo behind the ABM's seed distribution (\texttt{run-2026-07-04-15yr-foldin}) was frozen about two minutes before its own code was committed, so its manifest's commit field points at the \emph{previous} state of the tree. Its reproduction checkout is recorded in the ledger, and on that run, specifications must be identified by cohort-bucket count (seven for the thirty-year-only specification, eleven for the fold-in) rather than by the manifest commit, which is unreliable for exactly this reason. The consequence is worth naming rather than leaving implicit: for those two freezes the agent-based headline is not commit-addressable, and no later tidying of this disclosure can make it so. Anyone reproducing the ABM's 13.6\% seed mean or its 11.9\% frozen draw needs that rule, and it is the reason the two figures were once mistakenly attributed to the native-fifteen-year specification.

\paragraph{Reproducibility conventions.} Provenance details referenced from the main text are collected here. The ABM headline run's manifest lives at \path{abm/data/runs/run-2026-07-04-15yr-foldin/manifest.json} and is reproduced via \texttt{freeze\_run.py -{}-tag run-2026-07-04-15yr-foldin} against the fold-in specification's code; a manifest's recorded \texttt{git\_commit} is the pre-run parent of the freezing commit (the convention the replication package's technical documentation records). Post-freeze runs follow the spec-before-run convention: the grid, parity gates, and interpretive thresholds are fixed in the run script's header and committed before execution (for the floor sweeps, each spec commit precedes its own result commit: \texttt{ad52db6} before \texttt{d0ef130}, \texttt{651d1a0} before \texttt{57181b3}; the ordering is pairwise, and the second spec postdates the first result); where script and artifact enter the repository in one revision (the form test \texttt{hazard/floor\_form\_test.py}, the bootstrap), the header is the ex-ante record. The ordering itself is only partly checkable from this document, and I state that plainly rather than leave it implied. Twenty-seven standalone pre-committed specifications in the replication package's \path{specs/} directory fix the grid, the parity gates, the pre-committed expectation and the landing rule for each outcome branch before execution; the remaining tags indexed in Table~\ref{tab:runindex} rest on the run script's own header as the ex-ante record. For those, that a header was committed before its result is a property of the repository's commit graph, verifiable from git history, and not from this paper. The floor-sweep pair above is the only ordering quoted here by commit hash. Referenced artifact files not named in the main text: \texttt{wal\_anchor\_results.json} (WAL anchor), \texttt{floor\_sweep\_results.json} and \texttt{floor\_band\_cross\_results.json} (the calibration box), \texttt{theil\_data.json} (dynamic-fit decomposition), \texttt{vintage\_residual\_bound\_results.json} (vintage bound), and \texttt{ml\_comparator\_holdout\_results.json} (flexible-learner comparator).

\paragraph{The corrected mechanism-substitution (relocated from Section~\ref{sec:abm-danish}).}
The initial mechanism-substitution that this specification corrects had implied a far larger gap, \$925.5 billion, by feeding a U.S.-calibrated mobility function a Danish-style rate gap it was never estimated to describe; it is an extrapolation rather than a measurement, catalogued in Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}. The case for the Danish alternative is modest and anchor-signed: roughly $+\$61$ billion, 8\% of the benchmark, at the in-sample calibration point ($+\$28$ billion, 3.7\%, at the off-window headline floor), the counterfactual image of the lock-in marginal. Its positivity throughout the refinance-in-place sweep is a property of the zero-gap anchor rather than a finding, so the magnitude is the informative part. The earlier ``sign we cannot identify'' reading survives only as a statement about the bracketing Danish-level anchor, which asks a different question (what if U.S. households also moved like Danes) and answers it with a Danish leg that violates the floor both frameworks impose on the U.S. side. This does not alter Sections~\ref{sec:abm} and~\ref{sec:hazard}'s central finding (that loan-level and institutional mechanics, not household choice, explain the U.S. empirical shortfall) since that comparison never depended on the Danish counterfactual. What it does revise is the payoff-rule question: the par-payoff rule's institutional cash-flow cost is real but an order of magnitude smaller than the original extrapolation implied, and of the same origin as the lock-in marginal the paper identifies directly, and of the same size as that marginal at the in-sample calibration point where both legs were run, though about 44\% larger than the off-window marginal the paper headlines. Both legs credit retired balance at par; under cash accounting the market-value buyback credit reverses the gap's sign, so the case as stated here stands on the benchmark's own face denomination, with the incidence bracket priced in Section~\ref{sec:pathb}. The $1-P$ wedge the U.S. rule collects from a moving household accrues to the bondholder, and here the bondholder is the SOMA portfolio, whose earnings reach the Treasury as remittances: the cash-haircut reading hands that wedge to the households who move and takes it from general taxpayers. So the reversal prices a transfer changing direction, not a resource cost, and its welfare sign turns on the marginal value of public funds against that of household liquidity, neither of which this framework measures. One further omission is a price rather than a held-fixed feature of the U.S. leg, and its direction runs against the reported relief. Every figure here is ex-post, on a coupon stack originated under the U.S. payoff rule; under a market-value payoff rule the repurchase option is written into the bond series and priced into the coupon ex ante, so the same households would have borrowed at a higher rate for the life of the loan \citep{campbell2013,berg2018}. That premium is netted nowhere, and the only ex-ante rate term the transplant carries (Berger et al.'s roughly 20-basis-point equilibrium shift) prices the refinance-in-place channel alone. So $+\$61.2$ billion is gross institutional relief under face accounting and a held-fixed Danish product mix, not a net social gain. The second qualifier is not free: the interest-only crossing above sits at 34.3\% and Denmark's own whole-market, pre-window read is higher, so what carries the relief is not face accounting alone but face accounting on a transplant that changes the payoff rule and nothing else. The two reversals do not stack, and the reason runs opposite to the obvious one. Their dollar pools are disjoint (the \$470.7 billion of early face the buyback credit haircuts is Danish roll-off net of the scheduled component, so it excludes exactly the dollars an interest-only share removes), but they share a balance path: that early face is measured off the fully amortizing schedule, and at a positive interest-only share the deferred balance's own prepayment (\$9.1 billion at a 45\% share) adds to it. Summing the two cells would therefore understate the haircut, not double-count it. Their sum is not the joint cell, and no joint cell is run here.

\section{Pre-Committed Rules and Their Post-Run Adjudications}\label{app:verdicts}

This paper's checks are pre-committed, but pre-commitment fixes what will be measured, not what a measurement means once it exists: adjudicating an outcome against its rule involves post-run judgment, and that judgment has run in both directions in this paper: failures have been diagnosed rather than merely reported, and passes have been weakened rather than banked. A green gate suite is therefore not self-certifying, and this table exists so the judgment itself can be audited. Its domain is narrower still, and worth stating plainly, because a pass count invites a confidence it cannot support. The suite checks three things: that every printed literal matches the committed artifact it is drawn from, that artifacts agree with one another where they overlap, and that named spans are present and, where order carries meaning, ordered. It checks no premise. Nothing in it establishes that a run's specification was the right one, that an imported elasticity transports to this population, or that any identification claim holds; those are argued in the body, and they are what a referee should read the body for. Nor did it see the page: a suite of 108 green gates once coexisted with roughly 919 text items set off the physical sheet across nine pages (among them a full page of this appendix's own ledger and a row of Table~\ref{tab:headline}), which is why a separate check now runs against the built PDFs rather than the source. I report that here because it is the sharpest evidence I have for the claim this appendix rests on: these checks constrain what the paper may say about its own runs, and nothing more. Its inclusion rule: every pre-committed interpretive rule or threshold whose post-run treatment was anything other than mechanical enforcement, in either direction, plus the enforced rules that bound a headline quantity.
