Dear Editor and Reviewer,

Thank you for a precise report. I have implemented every point. Where a
concern was already met in the manuscript's supporting layers, I have surfaced
it earlier and in your own terms rather than only pointing to it; the two
structural requests I have implemented in the lighter of the forms available,
for reasons I give at points~3.1 and~3.3, and I am glad to go further on either
if you prefer. All edits compile clean (85 pages, zero unresolved references or
citations) and no headline figure moves. Section and line references (e.g.\
\S V.D, L446) are to the revised manuscript.

\section*{1.\quad Notation, units, and exposition}

\subsection*{1.1 --- Basis-point notation}

Done. Every \emph{numbered} reference now uses the single form
``\textsc{n}~bp'' (100~bp, 150~bp), including the previously hyphenated
adjectival form (``100-basis-point units'' $\rightarrow$ ``100~bp units'') and
the math-mode forms ($+150$~bp, $-380$~bp); 47 references now share one
convention. Un-numbered running prose (``one basis point,'' ``per basis
point'') is left spelled out, as those read as prose rather than as the
abbreviation you flagged.

\subsection*{1.2 --- Equation~1's $r$ versus the decimal rate gaps $\tilde g_{i,t}$}

The apparent collision was one of appearance, not units: $r$ in
Equation~(1) is a monthly rate \emph{level} that prices the annuity payment,
whereas $g_{i,t}$ and $\tilde g_{i,t}$ are annual rate \emph{gaps} that drive
the hazard. I have made this explicit at Equation~(1) (\S IV.A, L129): $r$ is
now defined as ``decimal monthly terms (the annual note rate divided by twelve;
a 3.0\% coupon gives $r = 0.0025$),'' stated to be ``distinct in both role and
units from the annualized rate gaps $g_{i,t}$ (in 100~bp units) and
$\tilde g_{i,t} = g_{i,t}/100$ (decimal) \ldots\ no rate symbol is reused
across the two.'' The basis-point pass in~1.1 additionally aligned Path~A's and
Path~B's gap labels to the identical ``100~bp units,'' so all rate definitions
now carry an explicit and mutually consistent unit.

\subsection*{1.3 --- Equation~5's continuous-hazard equivalent ($0.0693$ vs $0.0686$)}

Done, and traced to the code to eliminate the reproducibility ambiguity you
correctly anticipated. Equation~(5) as displayed \emph{is} the transform
implemented in the codebase (function \run{rothstein\_beta1} in
\run{hazard/literature\_hazard.py}): at $\delta = 0.065$, $P_q = 0.06$ it
evaluates to $\beta_1 = 0.0686$ (unrounded $0.068571$), the value carried in
the production configuration and rounded to $0.069$ in Table~\mlabel{tab:params}.
The $0.0693$ that appeared in earlier drafts is a \emph{distinct} object, the
continuous-hazard variant $-\ln[\ln(1-(1-\delta)P_q)/\ln(1-P_q)] = 0.0693$
(unrounded $0.069258$), which the simulation does not use. The main text (L446)
now states the codebase form and value explicitly and notes that only
Equation~(5) reproduces the reported results; the two forms differ by $0.0007$
and both round to $0.069$.

\subsection*{1.4 --- Conversational phrasings}

Both removed. ``The original \$925.5 billion figure \emph{was largely an
artifact of applying} \ldots: extrapolation, not measurement'' (\S IV.C, L258)
is now the direct assertion ``The original \$925.5 billion figure is an
extrapolation rather than a measurement: it applies a U.S.-calibrated mobility
function to a Danish-style rate gap the function was never estimated to
describe.'' ``Two things follow from this calibration choice'' (\S V.C, L479) is
now ``Importing Path~B's elasticity unmodified, rather than fitting it to the
benchmark, has two consequences,'' which names the choice and carries content
rather than announcing structure.

\section*{2.\quad Logical gaps}

\subsection*{2.1 --- Causal identification vs.\ accounting residual}

I agree fully: the $\beta_1 = 0$ switch-off identifies an accounting residual,
not a causal mechanism, and the paper now says so in your terms early rather
than only in the Identification subsection. \S V.D already states the marginal
``is not causal identification in the econometric sense,'' that no
quasi-experimental variation is exploited or claimed, and that ``what the
switch-off adds is accounting, at a stated price.'' I have added the same
characterization to the Introduction (L43): the decomposition ``is an
accounting exercise within a fully disclosed model rather than a causal
identification,'' with the elasticity's causal content ``inherited from the
external estimates that calibrate it, not established here.'' \S V.C (L448)
additionally reads the identified marginal as ``total rate-attributable
prepayment suppression, an upper bound,'' so any channel correlated with the
rate gap loads \emph{into} that bound rather than silently inflating a causal
estimate. I have also replaced the one ``footprint'' metaphor in the Conclusion
(L791) with ``measured contribution.''

\subsection*{2.2 --- Ginnie Mae extrapolation}

Agreed: 20.4\% of SOMA face is Ginnie collateral that cannot be read through a
conventional voluntary-speed lens. \S V.C now makes this explicit at first
mention (L450): the extrapolation ``is not behaviorally one-to-one'' because
Ginnie pools are FHA/VA collateral with a distinct borrower composition and a
servicer buyout channel absent from the conventional book, so ``Freddie
voluntary speeds cannot be mapped onto the Ginnie share as a like-for-like
behavioral prediction.'' The framework therefore does not model Ginnie
behavior; it \emph{bounds} the composition error, and the bound is one-sided
and small: the static Ginnie--Freddie differential gives \$20--\$47 billion
(2.6--6.2\% of benchmark, signed toward overstating trapped liquidity), and a
time-varying overlay built from the published monthly agency-CPR series
isolates a Ginnie-specific contribution of \$38.5 billion (5.0\%), inside that
bound (run \run{ginnie\_cpr\_overlay}). Structural Ginnie inclusion is held out
of scope for exactly the buyout-mechanics reason you raise.

\subsection*{2.3 --- Danish counterfactual anchoring and separability}

The selection of the U.S.-intercept over the Danish-level anchor is now flagged
as an assumption, not a fact. \S IV.C (L221) states that holding the
involuntary floor fixed while importing only the Danish moving slope ``treats
involuntary turnover and rate-elastic moving as separable channels; that
separability is motivated by the floor's involuntary semantics rather than
identified in the data available here,'' which is why the Danish-level anchor,
importing the two jointly, is retained as the bracketing case. Consistent with
that, the paper reports \emph{both} anchors and rests no sign claim on the
separability assumption alone: the gap is $+\$61.2$ billion under the
U.S.-intercept anchor and $-\$99.9$ billion under the Danish-level anchor
(Table~\mlabel{tab:danish}), with the anchor, not the refinance channel,
deciding the sign. I read the U.S.-intercept anchor as the rule-only
counterfactual (the only one consistent with the floor's stated semantics), and
the Danish-level anchor as the answer to a different question (what if U.S.\
households also moved like Danes), and I now say precisely that.

\section*{3.\quad Structural}

\subsection*{3.1 --- Estimator presentation: lead with Path~B}

I take the point that presenting the ABM first can read as a protracted
negative result, and I have sharpened the signposting so it reads as the
deliberate falsification test it is. I have \emph{not} physically reordered the
sections, because the ABM~$\rightarrow$~hazard order is the paper's
falsification argument (household choice cannot explain the shortfall
$\rightarrow$ a structurally independent loan-level framework can $\rightarrow$
the shortfall is therefore mostly mechanical), and reordering would sever that
logic. Instead: the Introduction already front-loads the 97.9\% result and
names the hazard framework ``the instrument''; I have added an explicit
expository-ordering statement (L43) --- \S IV ``reports the agent-based model
first as a deliberate falsification of the household-choice hypothesis, whose
failure motivates the loan-level hazard framework \ldots\ where the central
decomposition is established, not as a competing main estimate'' --- and the
ABM Results section now opens by declaring itself ``a falsification test of the
household-choice hypothesis, not the paper's main estimate'' (L175). A reader
now learns from the first page where the headline result lives and why the ABM
precedes it. If you would nonetheless prefer the physical reorder (hazard
framework ahead of the ABM), I will make it.

\subsection*{3.2 --- Variable notation: Table~3's $\beta_b$ collision}

Done. Path~B's burnout coefficient is renamed $\beta_B$, matching its cohort
state $B_{s(i),t}$; Path~A retains $\beta_b$ (on $b_{s,t}$). No coefficient
symbol is now reused across the two paths. The rename is carried at every site:
Equation~(3), the Table~\mlabel{tab:hazard-notation} row and its explanatory
note (L313), the parameter table~\mlabel{tab:params}, and all ablation
references ($\beta_B = 0$). The Table~3 note now records that ``no coefficient
symbol is reused,'' and flags the remaining case-only distinctions ($\beta_f$
vs.\ $\beta_F$; $b_{s,t}$ vs.\ $B_{s(i),t}$), which differ by case and are
harder to confuse than an identical symbol.

\subsection*{3.3 --- Accounting-basis presentation}

Done, on the principle of unifying early. The benchmark-consistent shared basis
is the headline throughout, and I have removed the scattered standalone-scorer
parentheticals from the four earliest, highest-traffic locations --- the
abstract, the Introduction (including the sentence that defined the 9.1-point
wedge there), the headline results table~\mlabel{tab:headline}, and the first
statement in the Identification subsection --- so a reader meets one basis
before any other. The standalone figures and the 9.1-point accounting wedge now
live in a single definitional paragraph (the ``Accounting bases'' paragraph in
\S III) and in Table~\mlabel{tab:bases}, which collects every hazard leg on all
four bases; the wedge's derivation and its exact cancellation from the marginal
are in \S V's shared-accounting-layer discussion. I stopped short of stripping
the standalone numbers from the deep robustness tables and their notes, where
reporting both bases is a reproducibility aid for a reader cross-checking the
technical documentation; I am happy to relegate those as well if you prefer the
stricter form.

\section*{4.\quad Presentation, notation, and reproducibility}

\subsection*{4.1 --- Dollar formatting}

Done. Outside of tables, every billion-dollar figure now reads in plain text
(``\$91.0 billion,'' not ``\$91.0B''), including the appendix's
superseded-figure ledger and the Danish table's explanatory notes; the compact
``\$91.0B'' form is retained only inside tabular cells, where column width is at
a premium.

\subsection*{4.2 --- Colloquial phrasing}

Done. ``This failed a sanity check'' (the fifteen-year fold-in discussion) is
now ``This produced out-of-bounds predicted values,'' and the accompanying ``a
large negative (implausible) value'' is now ``a large negative value, far
outside the empirically observed range''; ``the implausible bracket'' (the
allocation sweep of \S III) is now ``the extreme bracket.'' The one remaining
use of ``implausible'' is operationally defined ($|\beta| > 20$) and is
therefore a threshold, not a judgment; I left it.

\subsection*{4.3 --- Version-control identifiers in the narrative}

Agreed, and done. Every repository run tag, manifest name, results-file path,
script path, and commit hash has been removed from the body and relegated to the
reproducibility appendix. The body previously carried roughly forty such
identifiers, mostly as trailing ``(run \texttt{X})'' parentheticals; the claims
they supported are unchanged, and each run is now indexed by its result.
Headline-quantity runs remain in the basis-and-inputs crosswalk
(Table~\mlabel{tab:crosswalk}); the diagnostic and robustness runs are collected
in a new run index (Table~\mlabel{tab:runindex}), each row pairing a tag with a
plain-language description and a section cross-reference. External data-series
identifiers (FRED codes, the New York Fed SOMA series) are retained in the text
as standard data citations, not repository artifacts.

\subsection*{4.4 --- Danish counterfactual: operative specification first}

Done. Section~IV.C now opens with the operative Berger-recalibrated
specification and its result --- the two separately estimated channels
transplanted onto the U.S.\ loan population so that only the payoff rule changes,
yielding the $+\$61.2$ billion rule-only gap --- and develops it directly. The
superseded \$925.5 billion mechanism-substitution is demoted to a single later
sentence identifying it as the extrapolation this specification corrects, with
its provenance in the appendix ledger; it no longer front-runs the operative
method.
