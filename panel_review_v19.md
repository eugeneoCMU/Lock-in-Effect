# Part 1: Editorial Decision Letter

Dear Author,

Thank you for submitting "Mortgage Lock-In and the Federal Reserve's Quantitative Tightening Shortfall." The manuscript was reviewed by five independent reviewers, including the Editor-in-Chief and a designated Devil's Advocate.

## Decision: **MAJOR REVISION**

The panel's weighted rubric mean (≈68/100) sits at the Minor/Major boundary, but the decision is settled by three facts: three of four scoring reviewers recommend Major Revision; the Devil's Advocate reports five CRITICAL findings (which bar Accept under panel rules); and the required changes — while overwhelmingly *reframing and restructuring* rather than new analysis — go to the abstract-level claims and the document's architecture, which is Major-Revision territory by definition. The panel is unanimous that the underlying measurement work is credible, unusually transparent, and largely done. This is a "light Major": the analysis survives its own review; the presentation does not yet.

## What the panel agrees is excellent (unanimous)

- The **ex-ante cap arithmetic** (cap set 1.7–1.9× the Fed's own May-2022 projection; both mechanical components computable in advance, pp. 5, 58–60) is the paper's most durable and policy-usable contribution — every reviewer independently singled it out.
- The **transparency apparatus** — pre-committed runs, the adjudication audit (Table 27, pp. 133–134), the superseded-figures ledger, the retraction of the 88.5/88.7 "convergence," the printed against-the-paper results — is described by three reviewers as a standard other empirical work should imitate, and by R3 as something model-governance functions at central banks should read.
- The **Danish counterfactual repricing** (order-of-magnitude correction of the paper's own $925.5B figure; incidence bracket; series-vs-pool standardization insight, pp. 60–66, 82–83) is a genuine contribution to the mortgage-design debate.
- The **Fannie Mae external replication** at scale (+8.68 vs +9.20, with the too-wide gate honestly deflated, p. 39) and the wild-cluster inference ladder reflect current best practice.

## Consensus Analysis

Consensus is computed across the four scoring reviewers (EIC, R1, R2, R3); DA findings are tracked separately below.

### [CONSENSUS-4] — all four reviewers; author must address

1. **The manuscript is not in journal form.** 134 pp. total, ~87 pp. main text, 328-word abstract, paragraph-length Table 1 cells, run tags threaded through main-text prose, and a self-audit apparatus (Appendices A, G–O) that belongs in the replication package. (EIC W1; R1 writing comments; R2 W5, advisory `[FIELD-NORM UNVERIFIED]` as to specific journal policies but unanimous in substance; R3 writing/usability.) Target: main text ≤ 45–50 pp., abstract ≤ 150 words.
2. **The headline vocabulary overstates the behavioral–mechanical partition.** The β1 = 0 null's dominant level-setter is a turnover floor read from realized, *partly behavioral* turnover (conceded at pp. 1, 7, 26); the null partitions **rate-elastic from rate-inelastic**, not behavioral from mechanical. "Most of that gap was never about behavior" (p. 1), "mechanical," "trapped liquidity," and "I measure what it cost the Federal Reserve" all overstate what the construction supports — and the labels are what will be quoted. (R1 W1; R2 W3; R3 minor + assumption audit; EIC abstract comments.)
3. **The headline must be stated once, with its conditioning visible.** The paper's central number appears in at least four variants before p. 8 (+9.2, +5.6, [+2.9, +8.7], hull +3.5 to +13.1); the operative headline is derived in a robustness section (Section VII.F "is where the paper's headline marginal and level are actually derived," p. 66); the quoted binding interval prices exactly one uncertainty layer (the floor read's sampling error at δ = 6.5%) while the paper's own disclosed convention variants span **+0.9 to +13.2** (additive form +11.2; moving-share s = 0.25 → +1.8; Aladangady-anchored scaled null +0.9; PSA sweep +0.9 to +15.6). A reader of the abstract alone cannot state the paper's claim without error. (EIC W3/W5; R1 W2; R2 summary; R3 W5.)

### [CONSENSUS-3] — three reviewers, fourth silent

4. **Floor-form conditionality deserves headline-symmetric treatment.** The hard-max floor form halves the marginal relative to the additive/competing-risks convention (+5.6 vs +11.2), the paper's own mixture curve reaches +9.7 by s = 0.25, and its own floor semantics ("strictly-involuntary share plausibly well under half," p. 26) point away from the s = 0 endpoint it headlines. The defense is semantic; either bring external evidence to locate s (deed-record moving hazards; credit-bureau-matched terminations) or present the form-conditional hull symmetrically. (R2 W1 raised; R1 corroborated; EIC corroborated via Q5; R3 silent.)
5. **The ABM is over-weighted relative to its withdrawn verdict.** Roughly a third of the manuscript serves an estimator whose paradigm verdict the paper itself undercuts (cross-design crossing its own pre-committed threshold, pp. 68–72; no interpretable ABM null, p. 23; "not clean evidence about modeling paradigm alone," p. 5). Compress to a short subsection + appendix; strip residual "two structurally distinct estimators" rhetoric. (EIC W2 raised; R1 W5 raised; R2 corroborated; R3 silent.)

### Corroborated findings (two reviewers)

6. **Renormalization disclosure must be promoted and the bias sized.** The engine renormalizes both legs to realized holdings monthly, making recovery percentages near-restatements of window-mean CPR and biasing the marginal upward by an admitted, unquantified amount — currently disclosed in a table note (p. 52). Move into Section V.E's qualifications and run a compounding-consistent null (the Danish legs already implement dynamic counterfactual balances). (R1 W4; EIC Q7.)
7. **The title-level trade-off rests on an unmeasured leg.** The mobility/welfare cost is imported from the literature, never measured or bounded here (p. 57), yet the conclusion ranks the trade-off's legs. (EIC W4; R3 W1 — remedies differ but are compatible; see arbitration.)

### Single-reviewer findings (action-bearing, confidence 4)

8. **Few-cluster inference** (R1 W3): 5.9 effective clusters, max leverage 0.33; the choice of the Webb wild-t as "binding" over the wider CR2/CR3 Bell–McCaffrey reads ([+2.4, +9.1], [+2.3, +9.6]) is asserted, not defended. Defend by simulation calibrated to this leverage profile or quote the widest bias-respecting read; propagate the floor's sampling error to the null's 85.7% as well.
9. **Domain verification items** (R2 W2 + institutional notes): the Batzer et al. "$2.4T foregone capital gains" characterization; the 20.4% Ginnie share's as-of date/source; the $1,940.9B parsed-book reconciliation; "joint-and-several" for modern Danish series (likely wrong — verify against Berg et al. 2018); note that the 2024/2025 runoff-slowing decisions retained the $35B MBS cap; consider CUSIP-level factor paydowns to eliminate the four clip-zero months at source; "no loan-purpose field" → "no termination-reason field."
10. **Stakeholder additions** (R3 W1–W3, W5): a bounded household-side translation of the marginal (constrained moves × per-move welfare estimates from Gerardi et al. 2024); a one-paragraph taxpayer/remittances carry cost (trapped balance × funding spread × the WAL extension already computed); promote the FICO/LTV distributional tilt (1.23–1.26× below-740-FICO, 1.23× above-80-LTV, p. 44) from a robustness note to an equity statement; add a one-page policy box stating what a QT2 designer should and should not take away, and which accounting basis answers which question.

### Points of Disagreement — Editor's Resolutions

- **Household leg: demote the framing (EIC) vs. add the quantification (R3).** *Resolution: do both.* They are compatible: attribute the welfare leg explicitly to the household-level literature AND add R3's bounded constrained-moves band from existing inputs. The title-level "trade-off" language should be softened until the household side is at least bounded in-paper.
- **Surprise-share range: headline it (DA C1) vs. drop it from the conclusion (EIC).** *Resolution: neither.* The projection-relative result (lock-in ≈ half the genuine surprise; 23–49% off-window, 38–80% in-sample across disclosed allocations, p. 81) must appear in the abstract **alongside** the cap-relative result — the denominator-dependence is the finding. But its allocation-conditional width means no single number from it should be promoted as "the" headline. Present both denominators symmetrically, each with its band.
- **Danish "small cost": convention-laundering (DA C5) vs. honestly handled (R2).** *Resolution: split the difference on prominence.* R2's domain judgment stands — the rule-only, zero-refinance anchor is the semantics-consistent production choice and the incidence bracket is exemplary. But the abstract's "the institutional cash-flow cost is small" must carry its conditions (face accounting, production floor form, zero-refinance edge of a +$61.2B → +$256.8B band whose realized-implied anchor sits above it with four named confounds).

## Decision Rationale

The panel converged, independently and from four different angles, on one diagnosis: **the paper's measurement discipline outruns its presentation discipline.** The analysis is done, disclosed, and largely verified — but the abstract-level claims ("never about behavior," "small," the single tidy interval, the pre-commitment credential) each condition on choices the paper elsewhere concedes are conventions, and the document form (134 pp., headline derived in a robustness section, four headline variants before p. 8) prevents any reader from carrying away the defensible claim. Because the required changes reach the abstract, the title framing, and the document architecture — and because five DA-CRITICAL findings require substantive responses — the decision is Major Revision. No reviewer identified an error requiring re-analysis of the core pipeline; most Priority-1 items are executable without new runs.

## EIC Review Report

### Reviewer Identity
Editor-in-Chief perspective: senior scholar of monetary policy implementation and central-bank balance-sheet policy; editor of a top field journal in monetary economics (JME / AEJ:Macro tier). Scope of this review: journal fit, originality, significance, structural coherence, and strategic value. Methodological technicalities are left to the specialist reviewer except where they bear on editorial judgment.

### Overall Recommendation
**Major Revision** — conditional on a fundamental restructuring for journal form. The content is publishable at a good field journal; the manuscript as submitted is not in journal form, and the framing oversells one leg of its argument while burying its strongest results.

### Confidence Score
4 — I read the full 134-page text closely, including appendices; I did not audit code or replication artifacts.

### Dimension Scores (0–100)
- **Originality: 65** — The mechanism is conceded to be established (Fed staff said lock-in slowed runoff, p. 2, p. 10–11); what is new is the mechanical/elastic decomposition, the ex-ante cap arithmetic, and the Danish repricing — real but incremental.
- **Methodological Rigor (surface-level only): 72** — Extraordinary discipline (pre-committed runs, parity gates, adjudication audit, pp. 93–98, 133–134), but the design's own disclosures (no holdout months p. 8; no causal identification p. 45; sign forced by rate configuration p. 43) leave a thin identified object.
- **Evidence Sufficiency: 60** — Strong for the mechanical-majority claim (multiple constructions, pp. 17–19, 41–44); weak by the paper's own account for the marginal's magnitude (a 3×-wide interval that roughly doubles under a disclosed form change, pp. 46, 51, 77) and thin for the trade-off framing (p. 84).
- **Argument Coherence: 68** — Internally consistent to a fault, but the operative headline is derived in a robustness section (p. 66), demoted mid-paper, and must be assembled by the reader from seven scattered qualifications plus Tables 5 and 8.
- **Writing Quality: 55** — Sentence-level prose is precise and often excellent; document-level writing (328-word abstract, paragraph-length table cells, run tags in main text, 134 pp.) is far outside field-journal norms.

### Summary Assessment
This paper measures the Federal Reserve's $764.7 billion MBS runoff shortfall against its QT caps (June 2022–November 2025) and decomposes it: a mechanical null with the lock-in elasticity switched off recovers 85.7% of the shortfall, so the identified lock-in margin is a bounded +2.9 to +8.7 percentage points ($42.6B at the headline anchor), with a form-conditional hull to +13.1 (pp. 1–2, 41–48, 80). Companion results: the $35B cap was set 1.7–1.9× above the Fed's own contemporaneous projection (pp. 17–19, 58–60), and a Danish market-value-repurchase counterfactual would have relieved only ~$28–61B of institutional cash flow, sign anchor- and incidence-conditional (pp. 60–66). The question is timely and squarely in scope for a monetary field journal; the measurement is executed with exemplary transparency, including a superseded-figures ledger and an adjudication audit of its own pre-committed rules (pp. 93–95, 133–134). Two problems govern my recommendation. First, form: at 134 pages, with an agent-based model consuming roughly a third of the manuscript for a verdict the paper itself repeatedly disclaims (pp. 5, 19, 68–72), this is a research dossier, not a journal article. Second, framing: the title-level "liquidity-for-mobility trade-off" narrative rests on legs the paper does not itself measure, and its "trilemma dissolution" is conceded to be a calibrated ceiling (p. 84). The decomposition and cap-design arithmetic — the durable contributions — deserve to be the paper.

### Strengths
1. **A genuinely useful decomposition of a policy-salient object.** The paper does not merely repeat the Fed-staff observation that lock-in slowed runoff (conceded at p. 2, pp. 10–11); it partitions the shortfall into a mechanical base (85.7% at the headline floor, 88.7% in-sample; pp. 41–44) and a bounded elastic margin, and adds an expectations-based complement showing the NY Fed's own May-2022 projection already implied 75.6–88.5% of the cap shortfall (pp. 17–18). The ex-ante cap arithmetic — both components of the mechanical path computable in advance, cap set 1.7–1.9× above the Fed's own projection (pp. 5, 58–60) — is directly usable by balance-sheet policy designers. This is the paper's best claim to significance.
2. **Exceptional transparency and self-correction, well beyond field norms.** The superseded-figures ledger (pp. 93–95), the pre-commitment/adjudication audit table (pp. 133–134), the explicit retraction of the 88.5%/88.7% "convergence" reading (pp. 17, 44–45), and the public replication package with tagged manifests (p. 93) set a standard most empirical papers do not approach. An editor can trust what this author reports.
3. **The Danish counterfactual repricing is a real contribution to the mortgage-design debate.** The order-of-magnitude correction of the paper's own $925.5B extrapolation to +$61.2B (in-sample) / +$28.2B (headline floor), with the incidence bracket that can reverse the sign under cash accounting (pp. 60–66, 82–83), and the observation that Denmark standardizes at the bond-series rather than pool level — so the binding cost is issuance architecture, not a liquidity impossibility (p. 83) — sharpen Campbell-style design comparisons with numbers.
4. **Honest scoping of what is identified.** The paper states plainly that the marginal is a within-model counterfactual, not causal identification (p. 45), that the sign is forced by the rate configuration (p. 43), and that no outcome-holdout months exist anywhere (pp. 8, 85). External discipline is provided where possible: a full Fannie Mae replication (marginal +8.68 vs. +9.20, p. 39) and independent corroboration of the imported elasticity band (Graybill–Mangum, pp. 9, 36).

### Weaknesses
1. **The manuscript is not in journal form, and the gap is disqualifying as submitted.** 134 pages; ~87 pages of main text; a 328-word abstract (p. 1); Table 1 cells containing full paragraphs (pp. 6–7); run tags (`b5_joint_cell`, `floor_form_mixture`, etc.) threaded through main-text prose (pp. 30–34, 42–48); a "How to read this paper" referee shortcut (p. 8) that is itself a symptom. *Why it matters:* no top field journal can referee or publish this form, and the contribution's audience (policy economists) will not extract it. *Fix:* main text ≤ 45–50 pages; abstract ≤ 150 words; one results section; run tags and the sensitivity catalogue to the online appendix, which is already well built for exactly this.
2. **The ABM occupies a third of the paper for a verdict the paper itself withdraws.** Section IV's stress-test conclusion is undercut by the paper's own cross-design test (recalibrated real-covariate ABM recovers 59.3%, 76.3% at book composition, crossing the pre-committed undercutting threshold on all fifty seeds; pp. 19, 68–72), no ABM null exists so no differential can be reported (p. 23), and the abstract's two-estimator contrast is disclaimed as "not clean evidence about modeling paradigm" (p. 5). *Why it matters:* the reader invests ~40 pages (Sections IV, VII.A, VII.C; Appendices B–D, K, M) in an estimator whose evidential role the author caps at "supporting diagnostic" (p. 4). *Fix:* compress the ABM to a short subsection plus appendix, or remove it; the hazard-framework decomposition stands without it, as the conclusion itself notes the central contrast "never depended on the Danish comparison" and barely depends on the ABM.
3. **The identified object is thin relative to the apparatus, and the paper should be reframed around what is actually robust.** The headline marginal's binding interval is +2.9 to +8.7 pp — a width of 5.8 points around a +5.6 headline; the form-conditional hull is +3.5 to +13.1; and a disclosed functional-form choice alone roughly doubles the estimate (+5.6 vs. +11.2; pp. 46–48, 51, 77, 80). Meanwhile Table 11's own note concedes that monthly renormalization to realized holdings makes every recovery percentage "close to a restatement of that leg's window-mean CPR" (p. 52). *Why it matters:* the paper's strongest, tightest results are the mechanical decomposition and the cap arithmetic, not the marginal's magnitude — yet the marginal carries the title-level narrative. *Fix:* lead with the decomposition and cap-design results; present the marginal explicitly as a bounded residual with one consolidated uncertainty statement, stated once.
4. **The trade-off framing outruns the evidence, by the paper's own admission.** The "two-sided exchange" (TBA liquidity purchased with household mobility, pp. 3, 83–84) requires a measured mobility cost, but the paper never measures household mobility or welfare — it explicitly declines to (p. 57) — and imports that leg from Fonseca–Liu/Batzer. The conclusion then concedes the trilemma "dissolution" reduces to "the marginal is small," which "under the production floor form … is bounded small by construction" (p. 84). *Why it matters:* the title-level story is the paper's least-supported element; a referee hostile to the framing will discount the well-supported accounting underneath it. *Fix:* retitle/reframe toward the decomposition question ("How much of the Fed's QT shortfall was lock-in?"); state the mobility leg as literature-based context, not a finding.
5. **The headline is unstable across the document.** The operative headline (+5.6 pp at the off-window floor) is derived in a robustness section — the paper says so: Section VII.F "is where the paper's headline marginal and level are actually derived" (p. 66) — after the in-window +9.2 has been used throughout Sections V–VI; Section V.E requires seven enumerated qualifications and an "assembly" table (Table 5, p. 48) to state what the paper claims. *Why it matters:* a reader of the abstract and introduction alone cannot state the paper's claim without error, which is fatal for citation accuracy and for refereeing. *Fix:* a single results statement, one headline with one binding interval, with the demotion narrative (in-window → off-window) told once as design, not discovered mid-paper.

### Detailed Comments

#### Journal Fit
The question — how the U.S. mortgage contract interacted with QT implementation — is squarely within a monetary field journal's scope, and the cap-design material speaks directly to the balance-sheet-policy literature (Du et al., Krishnamurthy–Vissing-Jorgensen, Vayanos–Vila; p. 12). But the paper's evidentiary mode — calibrated simulation decomposition, no quasi-experimental variation, no holdout — sits closer to policy-institution working-paper norms than to what AEJ:Macro referees typically demand, and JME would require the ~50-page form it does not yet have. Realistic venues in descending order of restructuring required: *JME* (heavy compression, marginal reframed as bounded residual), *Journal of Money, Credit and Banking* or *International Journal of Central Banking* (natural fits for the cap-design and implementation content), *Journal of Financial Stability* (duration-extension/bank-fragility channel, p. 57), *Real Estate Economics / Journal of Housing Economics* (mortgage-design angle). I would not send the current draft to referees at my journal; I would return it for form first.

#### Originality
The paper is candid that the mechanism is not new (p. 2) and even that its null and Na et al.'s composition are "the same fact read against two denominators" (pp. 10–11). The residual novelty is real but must be stated precisely: (i) the elastic-vs.-mechanical partition of the *shortfall* (not of realized payments), delivered as a bounded margin; (ii) the extension of the shortfall accounting to the full window ($764.7B vs. Na et al.'s ~$370B through June 2024, p. 11); (iii) the expectations-based complement and the 1.7–1.9× ex-ante cap arithmetic (pp. 17–19, 58); (iv) the Danish rule-only repricing with incidence bracket (pp. 60–66). Items (i) and (iii)–(iv) clear an originality bar for a field journal; item (ii) alone would not. The paired cross-design validation device (p. 13) is presented as methodologically novel, and its candor is admirable, but its yield here is a hedge, not a result.

#### Significance
If the conclusions hold: (a) QT shortfall headlines against MBS caps are mostly uninformative about behavior — the caps could not have bound — which changes how the profession and FOMC-watchers should read runoff arithmetic, and gives cap designers a computable band (pp. 58–60); (b) the mortgage-design debate gets a priced U.S. counterfactual for the Danish rule, with the sobering result that the institutional cash-flow relief is small and incidence-dependent while the mobility relief is the larger effect (pp. 82–84). Audience: balance-sheet policymakers, mortgage-design economists, MBS researchers. That is a solid, citable significance case — for the *decomposition*, not for the lock-in point estimate, which the paper itself declines to defend as a point.

#### Structural Coherence
Title → abstract → intro → conclusion are consistent in content, and the paper is unusually free of internal contradiction — every tension is disclosed and adjudicated (Appendix O). The coherence failure is architectural: the headline is manufactured in Section VII.F (p. 66); Section V.E's seven qualifications, Table 5's assembly, and Table 8's three-layer uncertainty stack must all be read to know the claim; the ABM narrative (Section IV) is set up and then dismantled by Section VII.C. The paper *under*-promises and over-hedges rather than over-promising — a rarer defect, but a defect: after the seventh qualification, the signal is buried. The 134-page form is defensible only as a technical report; as a journal article it is not, and the author's own online-appendix banner (p. 93) shows the split already exists — the main text simply hasn't been held to it.

#### Title & Abstract
The title is serviceable but signals the mechanism (established elsewhere) rather than the contribution (the decomposition and cap arithmetic). The abstract (p. 1) is 300+ words, opens with "I measure what it cost the Federal Reserve" — which sits awkwardly with the paper's own careful standing paragraph that a cap shortfall is not a missed policy objective (p. 18) — and then compresses four layers of qualification into prose no abstract reader can parse ("the floor read's sampling error at my central elasticity; a wild-cluster interval on 31 clusters; the percentile read under-covers"). Rewrite at ≤150 words: shortfall, mechanical majority, bounded margin with one interval, cap-design implication, Danish result. Keywords/JEL are appropriate.

#### Conclusion
Section VIII (pp. 81–84) is the best-written distillation in the paper and shows what the whole could be. Two reservations: the trilemma discussion concedes its own dissolution is "a statement about a calibrated ceiling, not an independent finding" (p. 84) — that concession belongs earlier, or the framing should be softened throughout; and the 23–80% "share of the genuine surprise" range quoted in the conclusion (p. 81) spans so wide, under conditions the paper itself calls untestable (p. 18), that I would drop it from the conclusion entirely.

### Summary Assessment
This paper decomposes the $764.7B QT mortgage-runoff shortfall into a rate-inelastic baseline and a lock-in margin, delivered as a range (+2.9 to +8.7 points at the production floor form; hull +3.5 to +13.1) rather than a point. Its methodological virtues are rare and real: spec-before-run pre-commitment with parity gates, an audit table of every post-run adjudication (Appendix O), a corrected cluster-inference ladder with effective-cluster counts and leverage reported, an external Fannie Mae replication whose too-wide acceptance gate the paper itself deflates, and systematic reporting of results that cut against the headline. The core problems are equally real. The "mechanical" null is not behavior-free: its dominant level-setter is a turnover floor read from realized, partly behavioral turnover, so the partition is rate-inelastic vs. rate-elastic, not mechanical vs. behavioral, and the abstract's "never about behavior" framing overstates it. The headline interval prices exactly one uncertainty layer (the floor read's sampling error at a fixed elasticity), while disclosed convention layers — floor functional form (roughly doubling the margin), moving-share bracket (halving it), the PSA baseline level (+0.9 to +15.6 points) — span wider and are not composable. Inference rests on 5.9 effective clusters. The ABM and Path A legs contribute essentially no identified content. The paper knows nearly all of this and says so; the revision task is to make the defensible claim the headline claim.

### Strengths
1. **Pre-commitment architecture with an adversarial audit trail.** Grids, parity gates, and interpretive thresholds are committed before runs; Appendix O (pp. 133–134) tabulates every rule whose post-run adjudication was non-mechanical, in both directions — passes weakened (the 27-cell positivity demoted to a wiring check, p. 43; the Fannie envelope deflated, p. 39) and failures diagnosed. The claims-versus-code liveness audit (77 claims traced adversarially, p. 86) is a practice I have not seen elsewhere.
2. **Uncertainty layers are ranked, not merged.** The paper distinguishes sampling intervals (with coverage) from calibration grids (without), states the hierarchy explicitly (pp. 7–8, Table 8, p. 51), corrects a 37× understatement in its own committed bootstrap by moving to a stratum-cluster scheme (p. 37), and applies wild-cluster bootstrap-t with Webb weights, Bell–McCaffrey dof, and restricted inversion on the binding layer, with effective cluster counts (5.9 of 31) and maximum leverage (0.33) printed (Table 9, p. 52).
3. **Results that cut against the paper are reported and often given teeth**: the realized cross-cohort gradient at 4.5× the model's implied gradient (pp. 40–41); the cross-design ABM crossing its own ex-ante undercutting threshold (pp. 68–72); the age-standardized (5.51%) and independent Fannie (5.52%) floor reads both above the clean band, bracketing the marginal below the quoted +4.3 edge (p. 43); the scaled-null variant landing at +0.9 points (p. 47); the retraction of the 88.5%/88.7% "convergence" (pp. 17–18).
4. **The off-window recalibration and its decomposition.** Re-measuring the floor on pre-episode 2017–2019 discount cohorts, separating refinancing contamination by rate regime, and decomposing the +9.2→+5.6 demotion into window ($20.9B) and depth ($7.4B) components at matched depth (pp. 78–79, 125–126) is careful, self-critical measurement.
5. **Genuine external replication at scale**: the full Path B pipeline re-run on 17.6M Fannie loans reproduces the marginal at +8.68 vs +9.20 points under a spec fixed before the run, with the paper correctly refusing to credit the wide gate and crediting only the 0.52-point agreement (p. 39).

### Weaknesses
1. **The decomposition's "mechanical" baseline is contaminated by behavior, and the paper's headline language outruns its own concessions.** The null's dominant level-setter is a turnover floor read from realized total turnover that "mixes discretionary life-cycle moves and cash-out refinancings" with a strictly involuntary share "plausibly well under half" (pp. 7, 26); the null also retains the PSA seasoning ramp, itself voluntary behavior. So β1=0 partitions rate-elastic from rate-inelastic, not behavioral from mechanical — yet the abstract says "Most of that gap was never about behavior" (p. 1) and the intro "Most of the shortfall is mechanical" (p. 2). *Why it matters:* the paper's central quantitative claim inherits a category error at the level of naming, and the acyclicality assumption (a constant floor) means any rate-covariation of "involuntary" turnover is silently reassigned between legs (p. 30). *Fix:* rename the null "rate-inelastic baseline" everywhere including the abstract; state in the abstract that the 85.7% is conditional on a behavior-containing floor; and promote the mixture-curve result — that the paper's own floor semantics "point at the hull's upper region" (p. 42) — from a subordinate clause to the headline discussion, since it concedes the s=0 headline form is a convention, not a finding.
2. **The quoted binding interval prices one layer; disclosed convention layers are wider and include near-zero members.** [+2.9, +8.7] propagates only the floor read's sampling error at δ=6.5% (Table 8, p. 51). Meanwhile: the additive floor form gives +11.2; the moving-share bracket gives +3.4 (s=0.5) and +1.8 (s=0.25) at the headline floor (p. 30, p. 47); the PSA level sweep spans +0.9 to +15.6 at the off-window floor (p. 46); the Aladangady-anchored scaled null lands at +0.9 (p. 47); frailty attenuation reaches +3.7 at θ=1 (Table 8). *Why it matters:* a reader of the abstract will carry [+2.9, +8.7] as the paper's uncertainty; the paper's own exhibits show the marginal's economically plausible range under disclosed conventions runs from near zero to ~+13, and these dimensions cannot be composed (interaction −1.47 in the one joint cell run, p. 77). *Fix:* the abstract and Table 1 should state, in one sentence, that the quoted interval conditions on the production floor form, s=1, 100 PSA, and the central elasticity, and that named convention variants reach +0.9 and +11.2; alternatively present a single "convention-inclusive" outer envelope with the coverage-bearing interval inside it.
3. **Few-cluster inference: the "binding" layer may still be too narrow.** The floor read rests on 31 clusters worth 5.9 effective ones with single-cluster leverage 0.33 (Table 9, p. 52). The paper's own ladder shows wider bias-respecting reads: CR2/CR3 with Bell–McCaffrey dof give [+2.4, +9.1] and [+2.3, +9.6]. The choice of the Webb wild-t as "binding" over these is asserted, not defended. *Why it matters:* at ~6 effective clusters, wild-t itself can under-cover, and the quoted lower edge (+2.9) is the paper's key "does not contain zero" credential. *Fix:* defend the wild-t choice with a designed simulation calibrated to this leverage profile, or quote the widest bias-respecting read as binding; also attach the floor read's sampling error to the null's 85.7% level, which currently carries only a floor-range band (83.9–86.9%, Table 1).
4. **Monthly renormalization to realized holdings weakens every "recovery" credential and biases the marginal in a known direction — disclosed only in a table note.** The engine rescales the simulated pool to the realized (lock-in-affected) holdings path monthly, so recovery percentages "cannot compound error across months" and are near-restatements of window-mean CPR; the null is held to a balance path its own faster prepayment would have drained, making the marginal "an upper bound on the compounding-consistent one" (Table 11 notes, p. 52). *Why it matters:* this is a first-order structural asymmetry affecting the headline object's sign of bias, and it is currently in small type under a table. *Fix:* run a compounding-consistent null (the Danish legs already implement dynamic counterfactual balances, p. 102) to size the bias, and move the disclosure into Section V.E's qualifications.
5. **The two-estimator contrast contributes little identified content and its paradigm reading fails the paper's own test.** No ABM differential exists (the feasibility probe returns marginals of +270.4 or −105.3 points depending on how θ is handled — "neither is interpretable," p. 23), so the ABM-vs-hazard contrast is a contrast of levels, which the design says it does not identify (p. 41); the recalibrated real-covariate ABM crosses the ex-ante 50% undercutting threshold (59.3%; 76.3% reweighted; 16 of 50 seeds below threshold only after book-schedule re-scoring, pp. 68–72); Path A's coefficient does not survive a bias-respecting sign test (p = 0.093/0.412/0.241, p. 36) and its refit-and-resimulate interval spans −$4,459.8B to +$1,190.1B (p. 113). *Why it matters:* Sections IV, V.F, and the title-level "trade-off" narrative still lean rhetorically on a contrast the paper's own instruments have hollowed out. *Fix:* compress Section IV to the stress-test verdict and its cross-design hedge; strip remaining "structurally distinct estimators agree" phrasing (e.g., p. 54) that the diagnostics no longer support.

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Design
The question finally posed — how much of the shortfall does a disclosed hazard model attribute to its imported elasticity term, relative to the same model with that term removed — is well-posed and answered. The question the framing gestures at (how much did lock-in *cause*) is explicitly not answered, and Section V.E's fifth qualification (p. 45) states this correctly: the marginal is a within-model counterfactual whose causal content is inherited from Liebersohn–Rothstein's estimates. Discipline about this is mostly maintained; the drift points are the abstract's opening ("I measure what it cost the Federal Reserve," p. 1) against Section III.B's own standing concession that a shortfall against a never-binding cap is not a missed policy objective (p. 18), and the "mechanical" vocabulary (Weakness 1). The cap-relative benchmark is defended sensibly (only policy-committed quantity; a forecast benchmark would be circular, p. 19), and the expectations-based complement, with its allocation sweep and the two untestable conditions (the projection may embed lock-in; rate-path surprise), is handled honestly (pp. 17–18).

#### Data & Sampling
The Freddie 2017–2021 conventional universe covers ~51% of SOMA book face on joint agency×vintage cells — the paper is commendably precise that two-thirds is the misleading vintage-only marginal (pp. 15–16). The exclusions (Ginnie 20.4%, out-of-window vintages 33.7%) are bounded by observed-speed overlays signed conservative, with the overlays correctly labeled changes of estimand rather than corrections (Table 5, p. 48). Pre-window survival selection (34,734 of 75,000 loans prepaid before the window) is identified as attenuating and priced under a gamma-frailty identity, though θ is not estimable (p. 28). The off-window floor's 2018 leg is "clean by rate-regime construction rather than clean by test" — no mature OTM cohort-months exist to test seasoning (p. 126) — and both independent alternative reads (age-standardized 5.51%, 84% imputed weight; Fannie 5.52%) sit above the clean band, so the band's lower edge is soft in the direction that shrinks the marginal (p. 43). This is disclosed but should be reflected in how firmly the 4.99% mid-grid anchor and its +5.6 are quoted.

#### Analysis Methods & Uncertainty Quantification
The UQ machinery is the paper's best feature and, simultaneously, where the headline claim is most exposed. What works: paired central/null legs sharing seeds; seed noise correctly shown negligible (<$0.01B over ten paired reruns, p. 55); the within-stratum → stratum-cluster correction with the understatement factor printed (30.8–37×, p. 37); the inference ladder with dof and effective clusters (Table 9); the convolved layer honestly labeled a lower bound because one input is (p. 51). What does not: the interval's single-layer scope (Weakness 2); ~6 effective clusters (Weakness 3); the elasticity band having no coverage property by construction (pp. 7–8) yet occupying the same headline sentence as the sampling interval; and the ±1-point materiality convention applied absolutely in the form test and relatively in the transform test, a strain the paper flags but does not resolve (p. 28). The functional-form program (max vs additive vs mixture curve; concave transform; the −1.47 interaction) is exemplary as measurement, but it establishes that the headline's location within the hull is set by a semantics argument the paper itself calls a convention (pp. 76–78).

#### Results Presentation & Integrity
Selective-reporting risk is low: the assembly table (Table 5, p. 48) lines up downward corrections and upward counterweights; against-the-paper results are printed prominently (episode gradient 4.5× the model's, pp. 40–41; Path A's resampled mean at 78.3%, below the null, p. 113; the seasonal timing rule cleared by the elasticity-free null and by wrong-month rotations, pp. 131–132). Two integrity-adjacent concerns remain. First, *qualification flooding*: with four accounting bases, two floors, two forms, and per-exhibit conventions, the effective headline a reader retains is +5.6/$42.6B, while the defensible statement — a form- and convention-conditional range whose lower half holds every measured correction — is far weaker; the abstract should carry the weaker statement. Second, the post-hoc admissibility qualification on the externally anchored ABM variant is labeled post hoc (p. 72), which is good practice, but the same standard should be audited for the "refi-contamination" disqualification of the 2019 leg, which the paper itself re-adjudicates as "unadjudicable below gap ≤ 0 rather than refuted" (p. 80) while retaining the conclusion.

#### Reproducibility
Strong: public repository with frozen samples, committed artifacts, SHA-256-pinned tagged manifests, machine-checked liveness gates, and the spec-before-run commit ordering recorded (pp. 93–95). Defects, disclosed: two freezes ran on uncommitted trees so manifest git hashes do not name reproduction checkouts — including the run behind the ABM headline, which must be identified by cohort-bucket count (p. 95); Fannie inputs are code-only with headline statistics, so the external replication is not independently re-computable from the package (p. 39); Freddie raw files require registration (standard). Path A's inferential objects remain on spec v3 while its headline is spec v4 because bootstrap replications under the seasonal design are branch-unstable (pp. 113–114) — acceptable only because Path A is excluded from headlines, but it should be stated once, prominently, rather than distributed across table notes.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected
- **Circularity**: in-window floor calibrated on the population the marginal decomposes — conceded and remediated by the off-window program (pp. 41, 125); residual circularity in the seasonal profile (deep-OTM filter captures 98.2% of QT exposure, p. 132) is conceded. The Fed-projection complement's possible embedding of lock-in is conceded as untestable (p. 18).
- **Endogeneity/reverse causation**: no causal claim is made; the burnout state's endogeneity is ablated (p. 33). The floor-acyclicality assumption is the one live endogeneity channel and is disclosed but unpriced beyond the κ grid, which the paper shows measures dispersion, not cyclicality (pp. 30–31).
- **Overfitting**: guarded well — nothing is fit to the benchmark; the SMD exercise deliberately excludes QT-window CPR moments (p. 73). The real risk is its mirror image: with no outcome holdout anywhere (p. 8), "recovery" statistics are descriptions of fit, as the paper says.
- **Survivorship**: pre-window prepayment selection identified, signed, and priced (p. 28).
- **Category error**: "mechanical" for a behavior-containing baseline (Weakness 1).

Three institutional points to add or fix:
1. The MBS cap was retained at $35B in the June 2024 and March 2025 runoff-slowing decisions (which cut only the Treasury cap), with above-cap MBS principal to be reinvested in Treasuries — never triggered. The paper's constant-$35B assumption is therefore right, but this should be stated once; a referee who knows only "QT was slowed twice" will otherwise suspect an error (relevant to pp.14, 67).

Since the NY Fed publishes CUSIP-level holdings and factor data exist for those CUSIPs, a factor-based paydown series would remove the artifact at the source rather than diagnosing it. #### Framework Appropriateness
The mechanical-vs-elastic partition maps cleanly onto the field's turnover-vs-rate-response decomposition (explicitly connected to Chernov et al. 2018, p.8), and for a book that is 97%+ out of the money for the whole window (p.43), suppressing the refi S-curve and modeling total prepayment as floored turnover with a rate-gap suppressor is defensible — with two reservations. The turnover floor as a single acyclical constant is the frame's weakest structural assumption; the κ-grid and permutation diagnosis (pp.30–31) bound it credibly at ≤$5.29B of cyclicality.

### Summary Assessment
This paper takes the widely repeated claim that mortgage lock-in slowed the Fed's QT and does the thing the official record never did: it decomposes the $764.7 billion cap shortfall into a mechanical base (85.7–88.7% of it) and a bounded lock-in margin (+2.9 to +8.7 points at the headline calibration), and shows the caps were set 1.7–1.9 times above the Fed's own contemporaneous projection of achievable runoff (pp. 2, 41–44, 58). The self-audit culture — pre-committed rules, adversarial adjudication of the paper's own passes, Appendix O's judgment ledger (pp. 133–134) — is a governance artifact other empirical policy work should imitate. My reservations are about stakes and audience, not machinery. The paper's welfare paragraph concedes the welfare-relevant objects are the constrained moves themselves and then does not measure or bound them (p. 57); households get two sentences of acknowledgment against roughly 130 pages of institutional accounting, and the paper's own distributional finding — the marginal tilts toward below-740-FICO and above-80-LTV borrowers (p. 44) — is buried as a robustness note. ### Strengths (3–5, citing pages)
1. **The ex-ante cap arithmetic is a genuine accountability contribution.** Showing that the $35B cap sat 1.7–1.9× above the Fed's own May 2022 projection, and that both components of the mechanical path were "computable in advance of the cycle, as a band" (pp. 5, 58), converts a vague "QT ran slow" narrative into a testable statement about instrument design. The paper even supplies the number a cap designer would plug in, in the designer's units (floor read 4.177–5.800%, p. 60).
2. **The "question of standing" paragraph (p. 18) is exactly the discipline policy papers usually lack.** Stating plainly that a shortfall against a non-binding cap "is not by itself evidence that a stated policy objective was missed," and reframing $764.7B as a statement about the composition and duration of the decline, preempts the most likely misuse of the headline number.
3. **The incidence bracket on the Danish counterfactual is the paper's best piece of policy economics.** Recognizing that the cash-accounting reversal (−$89.4 to −$117.7B) is "the disappearance of a household-to-bondholder transfer, not an added resource cost" (p. 35), and that legs (2) and (3) of the trade-off are "the same dollars seen from two sides" (p. 82), is the kind of transfer-versus-resource-cost clarity that welfare analysis of institutional design requires.
4. **Appendix O (pp. 133–134) should be read by anyone who runs a model-governance function at a central bank.** A table in which the author's own passes are "upheld, claim withheld" and failures are diagnosed in both directions is a working demonstration of auditable model-based policy analysis.
5. **The WAL table (Table 12, p. 59) is the single most policy-usable exhibit** — 9.4 years against 3.4, with each estimator and counterfactual priced in the same maturity units a portfolio manager thinks in. It deserves promotion, not an appendix-adjacent position.

### Weaknesses (each: blind spot + why it matters + suggestion)
1. **The households in the title's trade-off are acknowledged, not treated.** Blind spot: the abstract says "the cost to households who could not move is real" (p. 1) and Section VI.A says the welfare-relevant objects are the constrained moves themselves (p. 57) — and that is where the household analysis ends; the equity channel is explicitly unmodeled (pp. 9, 26). Why it matters: the paper's own bottom line is that "the trade-off's binding cost is denominated in mobility rather than in institutional cash flow" (p. 3); a paper that concludes the cost lands on households owes those households more than a citation to Batzer et al. and Hsieh–Moretti. Suggestion: add one disciplined subsection that converts the identified marginal into its household-side image — the number of constrained moves the +$42.6B of trapped roll-off implies at the book's average balance, bracketed by the literature's per-move welfare estimates (Gerardi et al. 2024, which the paper already cites, is exactly this object). A bounded band with the paper's usual caveats would suffice; silence does not.
2. **Taxpayers and remittances are missing as a stakeholder and as a channel.** Blind spot: nowhere does the paper connect the retained low-coupon book (WAC 2.49%, ~$1.94T face, pp. 59, 128) to the negative carry the Fed runs funding it at reserve rates — the channel through which slower MBS runoff actually costs the Treasury and taxpayers money, during a period when remittances were already suspended. Why it matters: for a policy audience, "who bears the cost of extension" is the first question, and the paper's cash-haircut machinery shows the author can do incidence when he chooses to. Suggestion: a back-of-envelope carry cost of the lock-in marginal (trapped balance × funding spread × the WAL extension the paper already computes) would take one paragraph and the paper's existing inputs, and would give the institutional cost a fiscal denomination readers outside the MBS market understand.
3. **The distributional finding is buried.** Blind spot: Section V.E measures that the marginal's per-balance intensity tilts to below-740-FICO (1.23–1.26×) and above-80-LTV (1.23×) borrowers (p. 44), then moves on; the statutory assumability carve-out for FHA/VA borrowers (20.4% of the book, take-up unmeasured, p. 3) is likewise left as a bound. Why it matters: this is the paper's one original equity result — the lock-in margin is carried disproportionately by more credit-constrained households — and it bears directly on who a payoff-rule reform would help. Suggestion: surface the tilt in the abstract or conclusion as an equity statement, with its placebo discipline (which the paper already ran) intact.
4. **The policy-design discussion assumes the Fed wanted a binding cap, and treats the rate environment as invariant to the designs discussed.** Blind spot: Section VI.B evaluates caps as if bindingness were the design objective; an alternative reading — that the FOMC deliberately set a symmetric, non-binding MBS cap to avoid forced sales and keep communication simple — is never considered, and the feedback from the proposed alternatives (active sales widening the very primary-secondary spread the paper documents at 300bp, p. 4, and thereby deepening lock-in) is unexamined. Why it matters: recommending state-indexed caps or standing substitution rules (p. 59) without pricing their communication and market-feedback costs is the mirror image of the cap-design error the paper diagnoses. Suggestion: one paragraph acknowledging (i) the possibility that non-bindingness was intentional, and testing the design lesson against the FOMC's own stated composition objective — the 2022 Plans' intention to hold primarily Treasuries in the longer run, against which the MBS shortfall does measure delayed progress and which would strengthen the standing paragraph on p. 18 — and (ii) the partial-equilibrium boundary of the design space.
5. **The range-shaped headline is honest but not yet usable, and the paper knows its numbers will travel without their scaffolding.** Blind spot: the identified content is a range whose interior anchor is "an anchor convention rather than a central tendency" (p. 6), with a form-conditional hull that roughly doubles the margin under a disclosed alternative form (pp. 46, 77). Why it matters: policy consumers will extract exactly one of "$764.7 billion," "$42.6 billion," or "85.7% mechanical" and drop the conditioning; the paper's own retraction of the 88.5%/88.7% "convergence" (p. 17) shows the author understands how numbers detach from their conditions. Suggestion: add a one-page, plainly written "implications for policy" box — the counterpart to the referee's short path on p. 8 — that states what a QT2 designer should take (the floor band in CPR units, the scheduled-amortization path, the elasticity band as the behavioral margin), what they must not take (the point +5.6, the cap-shortfall as a policy failure), and which accounting basis answers which policy question (face for the benchmark, cash for reserve drain).

### Detailed Comments

#### Assumption Audit (explicit / implicit / paradigmatic)
**Explicit.** (a) *The cap-shortfall benchmark as the object that matters.* The paper defends the cap as "the only policy-committed quantity available" (p. 19) and then spends its best pages showing the cap was never expected to bind (pp. 17–18, 58). Both moves are right, but they sit in tension the paper resolves for academics (the standing paragraph, p. 18) and not for the public: the abstract still leads with $764.7B. (b) *Face-value accounting.* The choice is internally consistent — the benchmark is a face object — and the paper flags that the Danish gap's sign is incidence-conditional (pp. 34–35). From a desk perspective, though, the question practitioners ask ("what drained reserves?") is the cash question, and the paper relegates its answer to a bracket. A sentence in the conclusion stating which basis answers which audience's question would cost little. (c) *The turnover floor as "total turnover."* The paper is candid that the floor mixes discretionary and involuntary turnover and that the strictly-involuntary share is "plausibly well under half" (pp. 7, 26), and its own mixture curve shows the marginal roughly doubles as that semantics shifts (p. 77). The headline therefore rests on a semantic convention about what "baseline" means — disclosed, but a policy reader should be told directly that the conservative headline is a convention choice, not a measurement result.

**Implicit.** (a) *That "mechanical" and "behavioral" are separable in a policy-relevant way.* The null's floor is "read from realized, partly behavioral turnover" (p. 1) — baseline turnover is behavior too, merely rate-insensitive behavior. The construction is fine; the labels are doing rhetorical work ("most of that gap was never about behavior," p. 1) that the construction cannot fully support, and it is the labels that will be quoted. (b) *That the Fed's ex-ante projection is a clean counterfactual.* The paper concedes the projection may already embed lock-in and rate-path surprise (p. 18), making the expectations-based ratios upper bounds — good — but the same humility is not extended to the possibility that the cap's non-bindingness was a deliberate communication choice rather than a design error. (c) *Partial equilibrium throughout.* Every counterfactual (Danish rule, binding cap, sales) holds the mortgage-rate path fixed, while the paper elsewhere documents that MBS supply moves spreads (p. 4). For an accounting paper this is legitimate; for the design recommendations of VI.B it is a boundary that should be stated where the recommendations are made, not only in the limitations.

**Paradigmatic.** The paper's epistemology is accounting decomposition inside a fully disclosed model, with causality explicitly inherited from external estimates (fifth qualification, p. 45; p. 86). I find this more honest than most policy-relevant empirical work, and the pre-commitment apparatus (Appendix O) makes the judgment auditable. The paradigm's residual risk is at the interface: decompositions get consumed as causal levers ("lock-in only cost $42.6B, so contract reform is second-order"). The paper polices this internally; the proposed policy box is where it should police it externally.

#### Practical Impact (real-world application / policy usability / unintended consequences)
If the conclusion holds — most of the shortfall was never about behavior — the operational lessons are: (1) MBS runoff caps set at Treasury-symmetric levels are communication devices, not constraints, and future QT should either set caps against the projected scheduled-plus-turnover band (which the paper shows is computable ex ante, pp. 5, 58–60) or say plainly that the cap is a ceiling; (2) the elastic margin a designer must budget for is a band (+2.1 to +13.2 across the box), which is genuinely useful because it tells the designer the behavioral uncertainty is second-order relative to the mechanical path; (3) the assumability/portability proposals relocate rather than eliminate extension risk (p. 59) — a crisp, useful negative result. What the paper does not do: engage the choice the Fed actually faces (hold to maturity vs. sell; what redemption proceeds roll into; progress toward the stated primarily-Treasuries composition), or price the unintended consequences of its own design space (pro-cyclical caps, sales-spread feedback, the communication cost of a state-indexed rule). The usability gap is real but narrow: Section VI.B is one disciplined page away from being directly consumable by a QT2 working group.

#### Stakeholders (overlooked voices)
- **Locked-in households**: acknowledged as bearing the binding cost, never sized; the credit-profile tilt of the marginal (p. 44) is the paper's own unexploited equity finding. Weakness 1 and 3.
- **Renters and would-be first-time buyers**: entirely absent, though the paper cites Graybill–Mangum on housing-market tightness (p. 9) — lock-in suppresses listings and raises entry prices, so the trade-off's incidence extends beyond owners. One sentence would close this.
- **Taxpayers / Treasury remittances**: absent; Weakness 2.
- **Servicers and MBS investors**: adequately covered via the delinquency pipeline bound and the hedging discussion (pp. 55–56, 83).
- **Future policymakers**: partially served (VI.B); other central banks holding MBS-like books (e.g., those weighing active sales) would benefit from one paragraph generalizing the lesson: negatively convex assets embed a state-contingent exit option whose mechanical component is computable at purchase time.

#### Broader Implications (ethics, equity, other central banks, future QT design)
The deepest implication is stated almost in passing: the exit friction was computable before the cycle began (p. 5). That converts this paper from post-mortem to design input — QE programs that buy negatively convex assets should publish the implied exit band at purchase time, exactly as this paper reconstructs it ex post. On equity, the combination of the FICO/LTV tilt and the unmeasured take-up of statutory assumability (p. 3) suggests the mobility cost falls hardest on borrowers with the fewest alternatives, an ethics-relevant asymmetry the paper measures and does not discuss. On the international margin, the Danish exercise is properly humble (the sign is anchor- and incidence-determined, pp. 3–4, 82), and its real lesson for other systems is the locus-of-standardization point on p. 83 — series-level versus pool-level homogeneity — which deserves to be flagged as the transferable design insight rather than left inside the TBA discussion.

## Devil's Advocate Review

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's disclosure discipline is genuinely exceptional — every convention is swept, every failed gate is printed, and the adjudication audit (Table 27) is a model other empirical papers should copy. But a scholar holding the opposite view — that lock-in was the economically dominant story of the MBS runoff — can refute the headline with the paper's own runs. First, "mostly mechanical" is a denominator artifact: the cap was known-slack ex ante (set 1.7–1.9x the Fed's own projection, p. 58), the paper concedes a cap-shortfall "is not by itself evidence that a stated policy objective was missed" (p. 18), and against the only economically meaningful counterfactual the paper constructs — the Fed's ex-ante projection — its own arithmetic puts lock-in at 23–80% of the genuine surprise (pp. 18, 81). Same data, opposite headline. Second, the "mechanical" null is not lock-in-free: its floor is realized turnover read off gap-suppressed cohorts, and the paper's own reads show baseline turnover at 6.9% in a lock-in-free refi regime versus 3.8–3.9% in-window (pp. 7, 125–126) — the behavioral depression of turnover is booked as "mechanical." Third, the +5.6 headline is the conservative endpoint of a curve the paper's own floor semantics reject: with the strictly-involuntary share "plausibly well under half" (p. 26), the measured mixture curve puts the marginal at +9.7 to +11.2 (pp. 42, 77). Fourth, the "small" institutional cost is the zero-refinance edge of a +$61.2B-to-+$256.8B band, while the paper's own realized-Danish-data run (26.4%/yr deep-discount redemptions, p. 62) rejects the zero point. Every load-bearing minimization is a convention choice, not a finding.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths

1. **Housing-market conditions as the dominant turnover suppressor.** The paper's own Aladangady-anchored scaled-null (56% of the mobility decline non-rate) drives the marginal to +0.9 (pp. 47–48). A design that estimated the turnover-conditions relationship directly (inventory, prices, affordability) could absorb most of what the headline attributes to the rate gap — the paper implements the variant, labels it "upward-bias entry," and moves on.
2. **Turnover depression as the lock-in effect itself.** The paper's own floor reads (6.9% in 2019 vs 3.8–3.9% in-window, pp. 125–126) are direct evidence the "baseline" fell during the episode. Estimating a pre-episode turnover-vs-gap relationship across multiple rate regimes (2000–2019) and applying it to the book would price the floor's behavioral component — potentially several times the headline marginal ($83B per CPR point, p. 32) — instead of defining it away as "mechanical."
3. **Deliberate cap slack as the explanation of the "shortfall."** The cap was set 1.7–1.9x the Fed's own projection (p. 58), plausibly for Treasury-symmetric communication reasons. On this reading the $764.7B is a restatement of a cap-setting choice, and the only economic quantity is the $87.8B surprise — where lock-in is large (C1). This is more parsimonious than a 134-page decomposition of a number the FOMC never expected to reach.
4. **Observed-speed accounting for the whole book.** The Ginnie and vintage overlays show observed-speed substitution moves the level by ~$66B (p. 34). Extending that template to every segment (a pure runoff-accounting exercise, no hazard model) would answer the mechanical-vs-elastic question directly from realized speeds and eliminate the floor/elasticity machinery — likely landing near the +2.9–3.7 the overlays already show.
5. **Realized Danish behavior as the counterfactual.** The paper has the realized data (26.4%/yr deep-discount redemptions, p. 62) but builds its Danish leg from a transplanted flat elasticity pinned at zero refinance-in-place. Using realized Danish redemption behavior — even tax-attenuated — reverses "the institutional cost is small."
6. **Successive OMO-report vintages for the surprise decomposition.** The paper uses only the May 2022 projection's year-end levels (p. 17). The 2023–2025 OMO annual reports and Desk projections would date the surprise year by year and test whether the "anticipated" share was revised toward lock-in as the episode unfolded — a direct check on the projection-embeds-lock-in confound the paper declares untestable.

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

### Unexamined Premise

The paper's vocabulary — "mechanical," "baseline," "floor" — encodes the unstated premise that the *level* of turnover is exogenous and only deviations above it count as behavior. Every headline inherits this: the null "recovers 85.7%" only because the floor's level (an equilibrium outcome of the lock-in episode, as the paper's own cross-regime reads show, pp. 125–126) is classified as mechanical by definition. None of the eight dimensions fully captures this because it is not a measurement error; it is a partition convention that builds "most of the shortfall was never about behavior" into the accounting before any run executes. Relatedly, the paper presumes that a within-model decomposition of a deliberately slack cap is a well-posed measurement of "what lock-in cost the Federal Reserve"; its own disclosed sensitivity (marginal +0.9 to +13.2 across conventions) is evidence that the quantity has no convention-independent value.
