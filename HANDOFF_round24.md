## 1. THE PRIORITY — every correction points down, and nothing says so in one place

**Do this first. It needs no run and it is the paper's biggest exposure.**

Line these up:

| reading | marginal |
|---|---|
| in-sample calibration | +9.2pp |
| **off-window headline** | **+5.6pp** |
| Ginnie overlay (0.797×) | +4.4pp |
| age-standardised floor (5.51%) | +3.8pp |
| hull lower edge (round 23) | +3.5pp |
| naive composition of overlay × age-standardised | ~+3.0pp |

Plus: the **independent Fannie floor read is 5.522%**, *above* the clean band — which pushes
the marginal down again (`fannie_floor_read`, T2). And 68.8% of loan-months are censored at
the headline calibration, so the marginal is generated by a minority of the sample.

Every one of these is disclosed somewhere. **The assembly is never performed.** A referee
performs it in their head and concludes: *a small number, generated by a third of the sample,
shrinking under every check.*

**What to write** (V.E, one paragraph, no run): line up the corrections, state that they
cluster in the lower half of the sampling interval, give the naive composition (~+3.0) and
say why no composed point is published (round-22 B1's interaction of −1.47pp shows
corrections do not compose additively), and **commit to a posture** — the honest one is
probably that `[+3.0, +8.0]` is the identified range and the corrections concentrate low in
it, i.e. **+5.6 behaves like an upper-middle estimate rather than a centre**.

Counterweight that must appear in the same paragraph, or this becomes one-sided: item H's
three readings (Path A's own coefficient, the episode gradient at ~4.5×, the cross-design at
76.3%) all point the OTHER way. The paper declines each. §V.E already has the "sixth
qualification" paragraph confronting them — put the two confrontations next to each other.

**Do not** let this become a retraction of the headline. The sampling interval `[+3.0, +8.0]`
does not contain zero and the sign is structurally forced. The claim is about where in the
range the mass sits.

lock-in penalty, so the two candidate nulls disagree in sign.

preference:

the β₁=0 leg is pinned in 35.8%/14.3%. Disclosed in `sec:identification` since round 23.

## 7. Constraints that bind everything above

Read `HANDOFF_round22.md` §3 in full. The ones that will bite here:

- **Fix the `.tex`, not the gate.** Exception: a justified revision that changes pinned
  content moves **tex + gates + tests in ONE commit**.
- **Spec before run**, with parity gates replaying committed values bit-exactly. Round-22 C1
  exists because a precondition was articulated after a result was seen. **Items 1, 3, 4 and 6
  need no runs at all.**
- **Hedges are load-bearing.** Never weaken a qualifier. A hedge only has to travel with the
  claim it scopes — round 23 used that to cut the abstract — but if the claim stays, the hedge
  stays.
- **Run gates after every section, never batched.** Mutation-test every gate you add or
  re-point, and **revert the mutation in the file you applied it to**.
- **Zero-slack literals:** `positive at every` ≥6 · `$+3.0$ to $+8.0$` ≥2 · `11.06` ≥3 ·
  `76.3\%` ≥4 · **`$+3.5$ to $+13.1$` ≥3 (and `$+3.9$ to $+13.1$` must be ZERO)** · `60.2\%` ≥3
- **Never push Fannie data**; scan before any push (no `.env`, no secrets, no `fannie` paths).
- **Snapshot before destructive passes** — `paper/v18/snapshots/` (the 101pp v18 source was
  lost once). Nothing in that directory was tracked before round 23.

## 8. The habit that kept paying

Four of round 23's errors were mine and all had the same shape: **my reasoning was wrong and
the committed artifact was right.** A retyped constant from rounded console output; an
explanation naming the wrong window edge; an assumed artifact key (`us_trapped_b` vs
`trapped_b`); and calling an abstract sentence gate-free when it was pinned.

**Read values out of artifacts. Grep the gates before calling anything cuttable. When a check
disagrees with an artifact, suspect the check.**
